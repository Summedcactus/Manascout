MODERN HORIZONS 3 · #383

BORDERLESS CONCEPT · TRADITIONAL FOIL · VINCENT PROCE

ELDRAZI TITAN · BORDERLESS CONCEPT ART

Ulamog, the Defiler

An impossible presence at the edge of the frame. Vincent Proce’s concept-art Ulamog brings the Eldrazi’s alien scale to this week’s floating showcase.

Ulamog, the Defiler arrived in Modern Horizons 3 on 14 June 2024. This is MH3 #383, the mythic rare borderless concept edition illustrated by Vincent Proce, with traditional foil selected.

When you cast this ten-mana titan, a targeted opponent exiles the top half of their library, rounded up. Ulamog enters with +1/+1 counters based on the greatest mana value among cards in exile, then ties annihilator to those counters. Its ward asks for two permanents, reinforcing the sense that even interacting with it has a cost.

The original main-set #15 uses Chase Stone’s artwork; #383 is an artistic alternative, not just a foil of that image. Its concept treatment makes the creature’s strange silhouette the collecting decision before the finish.

MANASCOUT’S PRINTING

#383 traditional foil is distinct from serialized #383z in double rainbow foil and etched #476. Wizards confirms nonfoil and traditional foil versions of the borderless concept Eldrazi; this feature does not imply serialization, texture or surge foiling.

The slow void lighting and simulated reflections are ManaScout presentation effects. They do not animate or photograph the physical card’s foil surface.

UNDERSTANDING MAGIC PRINTINGS

One card. Many different versions.

What is an MTG printing?

A printing is a particular release of a Magic: The Gathering card. The same card can appear across multiple sets with different artwork, collector numbers, frames and rarities.

See Rhystic Study printings →

What makes a foil version different?

Foil cards use reflective treatments. Depending on the release, a card may have traditional foil, surge foil, etched foil or another special finish. Not every treatment exists for every printing.

Explore collector treatments →

How do I find the exact version?

Search for the card name, then compare the available printings. Set codes, collector numbers, artwork and treatment details help distinguish one release from another.
