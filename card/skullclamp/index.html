MTG PRINTING GUIDE

Skullclamp

Skullclamp is a Darksteel uncommon with an unusually consequential rules box. Its printing story starts with Luca Zontini’s 2004 illustration and a genuine original booster foil; it does not begin with a later mythic boxed release. The Warhammer edition and Yoji Shinkawa’s Secret Lair then offer different artwork, with very different finish choices. Start by choosing the picture, then verify the exact edition and surface.

When was Skullclamp first printed?

◇

Skullclamp first appeared in Darksteel (DST), 6 February 2004. The original is DST #140, at uncommon, illustrated by Luca Zontini. Available original finishes: Nonfoil, traditional foil.

RULES & FORMAT CONTEXT

Skullclamp in a deck

◇

Skullclamp costs {1}; equip costs {1}. It gives the equipped creature +1/−1 and draws two cards when that creature dies. A 1/1 normally becomes a 2/0 and dies as a state-based action, triggering the draw. Equipping is not itself a sacrifice ability.

Creature tokens can trigger it when they die, which explains its attraction in token decks. A creature that survives the toughness reduction does not immediately draw cards simply because it became equipped. Compare Living Death for a very different way of turning creature deaths and graveyards into resources.

At this review, Wizards lists Skullclamp as banned in Modern and Legacy; the catalogue lists it legal in Commander and Vintage. A collectible edition does not override format restrictions. Check the live legality panel before building for a particular event.

PRINTING HISTORY

From the debut to later physical editions

◇

Darksteel introduced Skullclamp on 6 February 2004 as DST #140, an uncommon illustrated by Luca Zontini. Both nonfoil and traditional foil are catalogued. A foil copy with this set identity can therefore be an original printing, rather than necessarily a later promotional reprint.

From the Vault: Exiled #12 arrived in August 2009. It retained Zontini’s art but used a foil-only premium boxed presentation and mythic rarity. That later rarity does not make the original a rare or mythic. Commander 2011 #260 returned to an uncommon, nonfoil deck edition with the familiar artwork.

Warhammer 40,000 Commander introduced Helge C. Balzer’s interpretation in October 2022. Ordinary #248 is nonfoil-only; the Collector’s Edition surge-foil record is #248★. These are distinct catalogue identities, not an optional surge switch available on every Skullclamp.

Secret Lair #1112, dated November 2022 in the catalogue, credits Yoji Shinkawa and offers nonfoil and foil. It changes the illustration as well as the presentation. These selected milestones are not an exhaustive list of Commander deck reprints, languages or later artist drops.

ARTWORK & FRAME COMPARISON

Which differences change the illustration?

◇

Zontini’s original image links DST #140, V09 #12 and CMD #260. Compare set symbols and collector numbers when the picture looks familiar: a Commander deck copy is not a Darksteel original, and the From the Vault surface is a separate collecting choice.

Balzer’s Warhammer card and Shinkawa’s Secret Lair are genuine artist changes. They are stronger comparisons for an artwork collection than treating every modern reprint as a new painting. The two Warhammer records share an artist; their difference here is the physical finish and exact starred identity.

For a Vampire deck, choosing an illustration is independent of the Equipment’s role. A different frame or finish does not change which creatures can carry it or how its draw trigger works.

FOILS & SPECIAL TREATMENTS

Choose the printing before its finish

◇

For the original image, compare Darksteel traditional foil with the foil-only From the Vault card. Do not judge the boxed-product finish solely from an animated scan: lighting, condition and product-specific reflectivity matter.

For Warhammer, use the exact 40K #248★ surge record. Ordinary #248 has no foil in its checked finish menu. SLD #1112 supports both nonfoil and traditional foil; it should not be called etched, textured or surge foil without a different verified record.

MANASCOUT RECOMMENDATIONS

Which printing suits your collection?

◇

Historical anchor: DST #140, with nonfoil and original foil as separate choices. It is our starting point for identifying the card’s debut rather than a claim about the best-priced copy.

Familiar art, premium product: compare V09 #12 against the Darksteel foil with real photographs. The same illustration does not promise identical reflectivity.

Alternate-art collection: compare Balzer’s Warhammer image with Shinkawa’s #1112. Choose the artwork first, then the legitimate finish offered for that exact number.

Deck copy: CMD #260 is one clear nonfoil reprint reference. Use current Cardmarket listings and verify language, set and condition; this guide does not assign fixed prices.
