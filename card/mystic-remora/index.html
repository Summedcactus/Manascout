When was Mystic Remora first printed?

◇

Mystic Remora was first printed in Ice Age in 1995. The original printing is collector number 87 and features artwork by Ken Meyer, Jr.

The Ice Age version was printed as a common and was available only as a non-foil card. Years later, Mystic Remora developed a much broader collector history through Secret Lair and Dominaria Remastered treatments.

MANASCOUT GUIDE

One blue mana for a brutal multiplayer tax

◇

Mystic Remora is a one-mana blue enchantment with cumulative upkeep and one of multiplayer Magic's most punishing card-draw triggers.

Whenever an opponent casts a noncreature spell, you may draw a card unless that player pays an additional four mana.

Unlike some other tax-based draw engines, Mystic Remora is not limited to the first spell an opponent casts during a turn. Every qualifying noncreature spell can trigger it.

That makes its one-mana entry cost deceptively powerful, particularly in multiplayer games where several opponents may cast artifacts, enchantments, instants, sorceries or planeswalkers before your next turn.

HOW IT WORKS

How does Mystic Remora's cumulative upkeep work?

◇

Mystic Remora has cumulative upkeep {1}.

At the beginning of your upkeep, an age counter is placed on Mystic Remora. You may then pay one mana for each age counter on it.

The first upkeep therefore costs one mana, the second costs two, the third costs three, and so on.

Paying is optional. If you choose not to pay the full cumulative upkeep cost, Mystic Remora is sacrificed.

THE FOUR-MANA TAX

Do opponents have to pay four for Mystic Remora?

◇

No. The opponent is not required to pay.

Whenever an opponent casts a noncreature spell, Mystic Remora gives them a choice: pay four mana or allow you to draw a card.

That four-mana amount does not increase with Mystic Remora's age counters. The cumulative upkeep and the opponent's four-mana payment are separate parts of the card.

In many Commander games, paying four extra mana for each noncreature spell is unrealistic, which can turn Mystic Remora into an extremely efficient source of cards while it remains on the battlefield.

COMMANDER STAPLE

Why is Mystic Remora so strong in Commander?

◇

Mystic Remora scales naturally with multiplayer Magic because its ability can trigger from every opponent.

A single turn cycle may involve mana rocks, tutors, removal, card draw and other noncreature spells from several different players.

Each of those spells can force its caster to decide between spending four additional mana or giving the Mystic Remora player another card.

Even if Remora survives for only a few turns, the amount of card advantage it can generate for a one-mana investment can be enormous.

FIRST PRINTING

The original Ice Age Mystic Remora

◇

Mystic Remora first appeared in Ice Age in 1995.

The original card is collector number 87, was printed at common and features artwork by Ken Meyer, Jr.

If you specifically want Mystic Remora's first printing, Ice Age #87 is therefore the version to look for.

Its original frame, Ice Age set identity and early Magic art give it a very different character from the modern premium treatments that arrived decades later.

REPRINT HISTORY

Mystic Remora had an unusually small printing history

◇

For many years, Mystic Remora had far fewer printings than its popularity in Commander might suggest.

A Masters Edition version appeared digitally in 2007, retaining Ken Meyer, Jr.'s original artwork.

Its physical paper catalogue remained much more limited until newer Secret Lair and remastered products began giving the card additional artwork and treatments.

That relatively concentrated printing history makes the major versions easy to distinguish from one another.

SECRET LAIR

Mystic Remora Secret Lair artwork

◇

Mystic Remora received a major alternative-art release through Secret Lair in 2022.

Special Guest: Kelogsloops included Mystic Remora as collector number 406, with artwork by Kelogsloops.

The printing was released in both non-foil and foil editions and gives Mystic Remora a dramatically different visual identity from the original Ice Age card.

For players specifically searching for a Mystic Remora Secret Lair version, #406 is one of the key treatments to compare.

DOMINARIA REMASTERED

Mystic Remora returns in Dominaria Remastered

◇

Dominaria Remastered in 2023 gave Mystic Remora its largest modern printing refresh.

The standard version is collector number 59 and features artwork by Jesper Ejsing.

Unlike the original Ice Age card, Dominaria Remastered Mystic Remora was printed at rare and was available in both non-foil and traditional-foil form.

The same set also gave the card separate retro-frame and borderless collector versions.

RETRO FRAME

Mystic Remora retro-frame printing

◇

Dominaria Remastered collector number 288 is the retro-frame Mystic Remora.

It uses the Jesper Ejsing artwork from the modern reprint but places it inside Magic's retro-frame treatment.

Both non-foil and traditional-foil versions exist.

The result sits between old and new: modern Mystic Remora artwork presented through a frame designed to evoke an earlier era of Magic.

BORDERLESS

Dominaria Remastered borderless Mystic Remora

◇

Dominaria Remastered also introduced a borderless Mystic Remora as collector number 420.

This version features different artwork by Ron Spears.

It is available in non-foil and traditional foil and gives the card a much more expansive presentation than either the Ice Age original or retro-frame version.

For players searching specifically for a Mystic Remora borderless, Dominaria Remastered #420 is the standout printing to compare.

SECRET LAIR COMMANDER

Mystic Remora in 20 Ways to Win

◇

Mystic Remora appeared again in the Secret Lair Commander Deck: 20 Ways to Win.

That version is collector number 1842 and features Jesper Ejsing artwork.

It is a non-foil printing with the Secret Lair set identity, providing another option for players who prefer the modern artwork without choosing the Dominaria Remastered release.

AVATAR

Mystic Remora Avatar: The Last Airbender version

◇

Mystic Remora later received a Magic: The Gathering | Avatar: The Last Airbender treatment.

The Avatar: The Last Airbender Eternal version is collector number 16.

It uses a borderless Source Material treatment based on imagery from the Avatar property and is available in both non-foil and foil.

This gives Mystic Remora yet another visual identity and extends a printing history that once consisted almost entirely of the original Ice Age artwork.

MYSTIC REMORA REPRINTS

What changes between Mystic Remora printings?

◇

Mystic Remora's printings now cover several very different presentations despite the card once having a relatively limited reprint history.

Differences can include artwork, frame style, retro treatment, borderless layout, foil availability, set identity and crossover presentation .

The Ice Age original preserves the card's 1995 identity, Secret Lair offers modern alternative artwork, Dominaria Remastered provides regular, retro and borderless choices, and Avatar adds a crossover treatment.

ART & TREATMENTS

Mystic Remora artwork, retro frames and foils

◇

Mystic Remora's relatively small printing catalogue now contains surprisingly large visual differences.

Ken Meyer, Jr. defines the original Ice Age version, while later releases introduce artwork from Kelogsloops, Jesper Ejsing and Ron Spears.

The presentation also ranges from the original 1995 frame to modern frames, retro-frame styling, borderless cards, conventional foils and crossover treatments.

That makes Mystic Remora an excellent example of a card where comparing the actual printings matters more than simply searching for the cheapest copy.

WHICH ONE?

Which Mystic Remora printing should you choose?

◇

For the original: choose Ice Age #87 from 1995.

For classic artwork: the original Ken Meyer, Jr. Ice Age version is the place to start.

For Secret Lair art: compare Special Guest: Kelogsloops #406.

For a modern standard frame: look at Dominaria Remastered #59.

For a retro frame: choose Dominaria Remastered #288.

For borderless art: compare Ron Spears' Dominaria Remastered #420.

For a crossover version: the Avatar: The Last Airbender Eternal #16 treatment offers another completely different presentation.
