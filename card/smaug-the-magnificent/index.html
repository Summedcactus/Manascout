The Hobbit original is not Smaug, Wicked Worm

Smaug the Magnificent debuted in The Hobbit on 14 August 2026. HOB #110 is the original mythic rare illustrated by Francisco Miyara. It is a distinct gameplay identity from Smaug, Wicked Worm, which retains its separate ManaScout guide.

Wizards describes approximately 500 gleaming gold #249 headliners, not exactly 500 and not a limit on every Smaug card. The headliner is English-only in Collector Boosters of any language. It is mechanically identical to the other Smaug the Magnificent treatments.

Original, Dragon hoard and Nasmith headliner

Francisco Miyara illustrates the original #110. John Tedrick illustrates the Dragon hoard showcase #229 and its surge-foil counterpart #265. Ted Nasmith illustrates #249, with its dedicated gleaming gold presentation.

Dragon hoard art and gold headliner are not interchangeable labels. Match the collector number and artist before considering the finish. A traditional foil #229 does not become the approximately 500-copy headliner because its image depicts a hoard.

Which Smaug foil is which?

The original #110 and Dragon hoard #229 offer nonfoil and traditional foil. #265 is surge foil only. #249 is the gleaming gold foil headliner; its special surface is not ordinary traditional foil. No serialization claim is made for that headliner.

Our first comparison is #110 nonfoil against #229 nonfoil, separating the original art from the showcase painting. For a premium finish, compare the exact #265 and #249 records, while using real photographs to judge the surface. No price, condition or authentication conclusion follows from this catalogue.

Treasures explain the gameplay identity

Smaug is a {2}{R}{R} legendary Dragon with flying and haste. Attacking deals damage to any target equal to the Treasures you control, and the upkeep trigger makes a Treasure. Those abilities identify this Smaug independently of its frame.

The One Ring guide provides related Middle-earth collecting context, but its Tales of Middle-earth release is not The Hobbit set. Neither a shared setting nor a gold-looking frame makes two cards the same edition.
