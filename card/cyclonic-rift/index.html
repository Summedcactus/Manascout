HOW IT WORKS

What overload changes, and what it leaves alone

◇

At its normal {1}{U} cost, Cyclonic Rift returns one target nonland permanent you do not control to its owner's hand. Pay the {6}{U} overload cost instead and “target” becomes “each”: the spell returns every nonland permanent you do not control.

An overloaded Rift does not target, so hexproof does not stop it affecting a permanent. It does not return lands, and it does not return permanents you control. It is a bounce spell rather than destruction; the owner may be able to replay a returned card later.

That asymmetry explains its role as a reset or an opening for an attack, but the timing and mana investment matter. Wizards covers the targeting distinction in the Ravnica Remastered release notes. All the printings below use the same underlying card rules.

FIRST PRINTING & REPRINTS

From Return to Ravnica to remastered Ravnica

◇

RTR #35 establishes Chris Rahn's original illustration and the card's Izzet setting. Commander 2014 #104 reprinted it in nonfoil; Modern Masters 2017 #35 offers the same artist in nonfoil and traditional foil. A foil advertised only as “#35” therefore needs a set code to identify it.

Double Masters in 2020 introduced borderless #339 with Chuck Lukacs' artwork. Commander Masters in 2023 retained Rahn's illustration on standard #84 and separate etched #485. Those releases expand finish and presentation choices without changing the spell.

Ravnica Remastered in January 2024 introduced Isis' artwork on standard #40 and retro-frame #313, plus Ryuichi Sakuma's anime borderless #419. The card is mythic in RVR, compared with rare in its original release. RVR also has a separate serialized retro record, catalogued as #313z.

About this guide

Cyclonic Rift has two scales: a two-mana answer to one opposing permanent, or a seven-mana overload that clears opposing nonland permanents while leaving yours alone. Its printings range just as widely, from Chris Rahn's original Ravnica scene to retro frames, anime artwork and the 2026 Mystical Archive. This guide identifies the releases behind those treatments.

ART & TREATMENTS

Ravnica art, anime and the 2026 Mystical Archive

◇

The original Rahn image is the starting point for a deck rooted in the card's first appearance. Lukacs' 2XM #339 takes the borderless direction. With RVR, switching from #40 to #313 keeps Isis' illustration but changes the frame; switching to #419 changes the artist and adopts the anime presentation.

The 2026 Secrets of Strixhaven Mystical Archive adds SOA #14 by Matthew G. Lewis, and Japanese-art #79 by Minoru. Minoru's #144 is the separate silver scroll foil entry. These use the SOA set code, not the STA code of the earlier Strixhaven archive.

Wizards explains the Japanese-language artwork and silver scroll finish in Collecting Secrets of Strixhaven. The Japanese entries are a language choice as well as an artwork choice; compare the image and language field before ordering.

A 2025 Secret Lair: FINAL FANTASY Grimoire version is also catalogued as SLD #1869. It is another alternative to inspect in the live archive if a crossover illustration is your priority. The shortlist above concentrates on the original and the major frame and finish comparisons.

FOILS & PREMIUM FINISHES

Etched, serialized and silver scroll are separate records

◇

For a traditional foil, compare the specific set and art you want: RTR #35, 2XM #339, RVR #313 and RVR #419 each have foil and nonfoil options. CMM #485 is etched only, while CMM #84 is the standard nonfoil / traditional foil entry.

The ordinary retro RVR #313 is distinct from the serialized version recorded as #313z. The “z” is a catalogue suffix, not a different spell or a printed serial number. Check the physical serial stamp when assessing an offered serialized card. Wizards identifies the serialized retro treatment as double rainbow foil in its Ravnica Remastered collecting guide.

For the new Archive art, SOA #79 is nonfoil; #144 is the silver scroll foil version. Do not assume a Japanese-art listing has a traditional foil option. ManaScout's animated foil preview is illustrative and cannot authenticate a finish or serial stamp.

WHICH ONE?

Which Cyclonic Rift printing should you choose?

◇

For the original: choose RTR #35. To keep Rahn's art with a different set identity, compare the Commander 2014, Modern Masters 2017 and Commander Masters reprints.

For a deck copy: compare standard nonfoils in similar condition. Collector treatments do not improve overload; use current price fields and actual listings to judge the difference.

For a retro deck: RVR #313 provides the frame in nonfoil or traditional foil. Only seek #313z if the physical serialization is part of your collecting goal.

For alternative art: put Lukacs #339 beside Sakuma #419 and the SOA Archive choices. If choosing Minoru's artwork, decide between Japanese nonfoil #79 and silver scroll #144 explicitly.
