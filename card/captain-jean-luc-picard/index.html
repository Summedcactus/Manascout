Captain Jean-Luc Picard

Captain Jean-Luc Picard enters Magic’s Star Trek release as a crew-building legendary creature, but his collector choices extend beyond the standard card. The original Anna Pavleeva illustration, Erikas Perl’s LCARS treatment and Irvin Rodriguez’s signed headliner serve different purposes. This guide identifies the announced printings by set and collector number, then separates the artwork, frame and finish choices that a listing simply labelled “foil Picard” can conceal.

When was Captain Jean-Luc Picard first printed?

◇

Captain Jean-Luc Picard’s first release is Star Trek (TRK), scheduled for global tabletop release on 13 November 2026. The original standard-frame record is TRK #7, a mythic rare illustrated by Anna Pavleeva, with nonfoil and traditional foil versions.

Collector shortcut: #363 is the LCARS showcase version by Erikas Perl; #497 is its surge foil counterpart. The signed, borderless headliner is #1701D, illustrated by Irvin Rodriguez. Keep the letter D when recording that collector number.

Ahead of release: this guide was checked on 4 November 2026. These are announced catalogue entries, not evidence that the set has already launched. Availability and legality should be checked again before ordering or playing.

HOW IT WORKS

An Officer engine with a white colour identity

◇

Picard costs {2}{W} and is a 2/3 legendary Human Officer. His colour identity is white: creating red Officer tokens does not put a red mana symbol into his colour identity. His static bonus benefits your other creatures, making the tokens useful both as a board presence and as a resource for his final ability.

Casting a creature or Spacecraft spell produces a red Officer token. This is a cast trigger, so simply putting a creature onto the battlefield is different. Tapping seven untapped Officers searches for a Spacecraft and puts it directly onto the battlefield with seven charge counters.

The official release notes confirm that the token trigger can resolve even if its spell is countered, and that Picard and newly arrived Officers can help pay the seven-Officer cost. The ability does not use the tap symbol on those creatures. This is useful context for choosing a deck copy, without making the premium versions mechanically stronger.

FIRST PRINTING & RELEASE VARIANTS

One announced release family, four collector records

◇

The four verified English records belong to TRK and carry the same scheduled release date. #7 is the main-set baseline; #363 and #497 change the presentation and finish; #1701D is the signed headliner. They are release variants of the same card, rather than a sequence of later-set reprints.

The showcase number is not a second gameplay identity. Equally, a card depicting another incarnation of Picard is not automatically a reprint of Captain Jean-Luc Picard. Match the complete card name before adding an edition to a collection checklist.

The shortlist below covers the records verified for this review. It does not assign an extra promo or serialized edition merely because the set has other promotional products. Future catalogue additions belong in the live explorer and should be checked before expanding the editorial history.

ARTWORK & TREATMENTS

Pavleeva’s original, LCARS design or a signed portrait?

◇

Standard #7: Anna Pavleeva supplies the original illustration in the regular Magic frame. It is the clearest reference point if you want the first main-set presentation, or a readable deck copy without choosing a special frame.

LCARS #363 and #497: Erikas Perl is credited on both records. LCARS is the Starfleet computer-interface frame treatment described in Wizards’ collecting guide. The artist change from #7 gives you a real artwork comparison; the difference between #363 and #497 is a separate finish-specific collector record, not a later reprint.

Headliner #1701D: Irvin Rodriguez’s borderless portrait is catalogued as a textless signed headliner. Its illustrator credit and the actor’s autograph describe different contributions. For a display collection, compare the portrait itself as well as the signature placement and surface condition.

FOILS & PREMIUM FINISHES

Traditional foil, surge foil and the signed headliner

◇

TRK #7 and LCARS #363 each offer nonfoil and traditional foil. If you want Perl’s LCARS presentation without a reflective surface, #363 nonfoil is the relevant option. Selecting traditional foil on #363 does not turn it into the surge version.

TRK #497 is the surge foil LCARS record. Wizards places surge foil LCARS cards in Collector Boosters. That is a finish and product distinction worth checking when a listing uses only “showcase foil.” #1701D is foil-only and belongs to the signed headliner category; the catalogue’s broad foil label should not be treated as proof of a specific surge finish.

For an autograph purchase, require photographs of the actual copy and the identifying number. A digital render cannot establish the signature’s condition, authenticity or placement. A collector number identifies a version; it is not proof that an individual copy is serialized.

WHICH PRINTING?

Which Captain Jean-Luc Picard printing should you choose?

◇

For the original: start with TRK #7. Nonfoil and traditional foil let you choose the surface while keeping Pavleeva’s original presentation.

For a Starfleet-themed collection: compare #363 nonfoil with #497 surge foil. They make the LCARS frame the central choice, with the finish considered separately. #363 traditional foil is a third option between those two.

For a deck copy: compare #7 and #363 in the same condition and finish. Keep readability and your preferred frame ahead of an assumed premium; this guide makes no live price ranking.

For an autograph collectible: use the exact #1701D record and inspect the actual signed card. Do not substitute another Picard card, another collector number or an ordinary foil sold with a vague signature description.

◇ MANASCOUT PERSPECTIVE

ManaScout pick: compare #7 with LCARS #363 first

◇

Our starting pair is #7 nonfoil and #363 nonfoil. Holding the finish constant makes the meaningful comparison the artwork and LCARS presentation. If LCARS is the reason you want the card, consider #497 next for the surge finish; an autograph version is not necessary to appreciate the frame design.

For a focused Star Trek binder, #363 is our presentation-led starting point: it communicates the franchise through the interface frame while retaining printed rules. Choose #7 instead if documenting the original main-set illustration matters most. These are editorial preferences, not claims about investment value or current prices.
