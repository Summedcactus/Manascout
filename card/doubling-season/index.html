When was Doubling Season first printed?

◇

Doubling Season was first printed in Ravnica: City of Guilds in 2005. The original card is collector number 158 and features artwork by Wayne Reynolds.

Since that first release, Doubling Season has become one of Magic's most recognisable token-and-counter enchantments and has received reprints ranging from traditional Masters-set cards to borderless art, showcase treatments and premium foils.

MANASCOUT GUIDE

Twice the tokens. Twice the counters.

◇

Doubling Season is a five-mana green enchantment built around one of Magic's most satisfying concepts: if one is good, two must be better.

If an effect would create one or more tokens under your control, Doubling Season causes that effect to create twice that many tokens instead.

If an effect would put one or more counters on a permanent you control, it similarly puts twice that many counters on that permanent.

That gives the card applications across token strategies, +1/+1 counter decks, planeswalkers and numerous other permanents that enter or accumulate counters.

HOW IT WORKS

What exactly does Doubling Season double?

◇

Doubling Season is a replacement effect. It changes how many tokens an effect creates or how many counters an effect puts onto a permanent you control.

It applies to permanents that enter the battlefield with counters, which is especially important for cards such as planeswalkers and Battles.

A planeswalker entering under your control therefore enters with twice its normal number of loyalty counters while Doubling Season is applying.

However, adding loyalty counters as the cost of activating a planeswalker ability is not doubled, because those counters are being added as a cost rather than by an effect.

DOUBLE THE DOUBLING

Do multiple Doubling Seasons stack?

◇

Yes. Multiple Doubling Seasons can apply to the same event.

Two Doubling Seasons turn the original number of affected tokens or counters into four times that amount.

Three Doubling Seasons can turn it into eight times the original amount.

That exponential growth is one reason the card is so closely associated with the kind of spectacular board states that Commander decks can produce.

FIRST PRINTING

The original Ravnica Doubling Season

◇

Doubling Season first appeared in Ravnica: City of Guilds in 2005.

The original printing is a rare, carries collector number 158 and features artwork by Wayne Reynolds.

If you specifically want Doubling Season's first printing, the Ravnica card is therefore the version to look for rather than one of its many later Masters, showcase or premium releases.

It also predates Doubling Season's long association with Commander, planeswalker-heavy decks and premium reprints, giving the original a very different identity from many modern versions.

DOUBLING SEASON REPRINTS

How has Doubling Season been reprinted?

◇

Doubling Season's later printing history is much broader than a simple sequence of identical reprints.

Later releases have changed artwork, rarity presentation, frame design, foil finish, borderless styling and showcase treatment .

The card has appeared in products including Modern Masters, Battlebond, Double Masters, Commander Masters, Wilds of Eldraine: Enchanting Tales and Foundations .

More recent versions have pushed even further into collector-focused treatments, meaning two Doubling Seasons can now look almost completely unrelated while performing exactly the same job.

DOUBLE MASTERS

The borderless Double Masters Doubling Season

◇

Double Masters in 2020 gave Doubling Season one of its first major premium borderless-style treatments.

The special version is collector number 350 and features artwork by Ron Spencer.

It was produced in both foil and non-foil forms and presents Doubling Season very differently from the traditional Ravnica-era card.

For players who prefer dramatic artwork filling more of the card rather than a conventional frame, the Double Masters version remains one of Doubling Season's most recognisable alternatives.

COMMANDER MASTERS

Doubling Season in Commander Masters

◇

Doubling Season returned in Commander Masters in 2023 as a mythic rare.

The regular version uses the Chuck Lukacs artwork associated with several earlier reprints of the card.

Commander Masters also produced collector number 557, a foil-etched premium treatment of Doubling Season.

The set was a natural home for the card given Doubling Season's long-standing popularity in token, counter and planeswalker strategies in Commander.

ENCHANTING TALES

Wilds of Eldraine Doubling Season art

◇

Wilds of Eldraine: Enchanting Tales gave Doubling Season another major visual overhaul in 2023.

The Enchanting Tales collection included multiple Doubling Season treatments, including collector number 52 with artwork by Eva Eskelinen.

Another version, collector number 81, features artwork by Kemonomichi and a highly distinctive borderless showcase presentation.

That Kemonomichi artwork was also used for the especially premium collector number 101 confetti-foil version.

CONFETTI FOIL

Doubling Season confetti foil

◇

The Wilds of Eldraine version numbered 101 takes the Kemonomichi artwork and combines it with a confetti-foil treatment.

Rather than being simply another traditional foil, the finish gives the card a deliberately extravagant collector presentation.

That makes it one of the most visually distinctive Doubling Seasons to compare against the restrained original Ravnica card.

FOUNDATIONS

Doubling Season returns in Foundations

◇

Doubling Season returned again in Magic: The Gathering Foundations in 2024.

The main-set version is collector number 216 and uses the Chuck Lukacs artwork familiar from several earlier reprints.

Foundations is particularly important to Doubling Season's printing history because the set did not stop with a normal reprint — it also gave the card one of its most elaborate showcase treatments.

JAPAN SHOWCASE

Doubling Season Japan Showcase

◇

Doubling Season was selected as one of the Japan Showcase cards in Foundations.

Collector number 428 features new artwork by Kawasumi inside the distinctive Japan Showcase frame.

Japan Showcase cards were created with artwork by Japanese artists and illustrators and were available through Foundations Collector Boosters.

The treatment transforms Doubling Season into something completely removed from the visual identity of the original Wayne Reynolds Ravnica card.

FRACTURE FOIL

Doubling Season Japan Showcase fracture foil

◇

Foundations also gave the Kawasumi Japan Showcase artwork an even more premium treatment.

Collector number 438 is the fracture-foil Japan Showcase Doubling Season.

It uses the same underlying Kawasumi artwork as the regular Japan Showcase version but applies the distinctive fracture foil finish.

For collectors comparing the premium end of Doubling Season's catalogue, #428 and #438 make an especially useful pair: same showcase concept, substantially different finish.

2026 SPOTLIGHT

Doubling Season Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles printing

◇

Doubling Season received another completely new look in Magic: The Gathering | Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles in 2026.

The Source Material version is collector number 11 and features artwork by Kevin Eastman, co-creator of Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles.

It is a borderless Source Material treatment that uses imagery rooted in TMNT's comic-book history rather than conventional Magic worldbuilding.

Non-foil and traditional-foil versions exist, making the TMNT printing another distinct branch in Doubling Season's increasingly varied collector history.

ART & TREATMENTS

Doubling Season artwork, borderless cards and foils

◇

Doubling Season now has one of the more diverse visual histories among popular green Commander enchantments.

Wayne Reynolds defines the 2005 original, while later releases have introduced artwork from artists including Chuck Lukacs, Richard Wright, Ron Spencer, Eva Eskelinen, Kemonomichi, Kawasumi and Kevin Eastman.

The treatment choices are just as varied: traditional frames, borderless versions, foil-etched cards, confetti foil, Japan Showcase, fracture foil and crossover Source Material art.

There is therefore no single objective best Doubling Season art. Comparing the actual printings is far more useful than choosing by card name alone.

WHICH ONE?

Which Doubling Season printing should you choose?

◇

For the original: choose Ravnica: City of Guilds #158 from 2005.

For a deck copy: compare modern reprints and current prices if your priority is simply playing Doubling Season.

For classic Magic artwork: start with Wayne Reynolds' original Ravnica illustration.

For borderless art: compare Double Masters and the later premium showcase versions.

For Enchanting Tales: compare the Eva Eskelinen and Kemonomichi versions from Wilds of Eldraine.

For a premium foil: compare the Wilds of Eldraine confetti foil with the Foundations fracture foil.

For Japanese showcase art: look at Kawasumi's Foundations Japan Showcase version.

For something completely different: the 2026 Kevin Eastman TMNT Source Material card gives Doubling Season a comic-book identity unlike any of its earlier printings.
