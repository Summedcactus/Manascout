MTG PRINTING GUIDE

Swan Song

Swan Song began in Theros as a rare with Peter Mohrbacher’s artwork and both nonfoil and foil. Its later editions are worth separating by artist: familiar Commander reprints, Allen Douglas’s Secret Lair, Axel Sauerwald’s Middle-earth image and Kelogsloops’ later Secret Lair interpretation. Not every alternate-art Swan Song has a foil option, so “special version” is too imprecise when choosing an exact copy.

When was Swan Song first printed?

◇

Swan Song first appeared in Theros (THS), 27 September 2013. The original is THS #65, at rare, illustrated by Peter Mohrbacher. Available original finishes: Nonfoil, traditional foil.

RULES & FORMAT CONTEXT

Swan Song in a deck

◇

For {U}, Swan Song counters an enchantment, instant or sorcery spell; that spell’s controller creates a 2/2 blue Bird token with flying. It is not a universal one-mana counterspell, and it does not remove an enchantment already on the battlefield.

The Bird is a real drawback rather than decoration. The original rulings also explain that a legally targeted spell that cannot be countered still produces the token when Swan Song resolves. Compare Counterspell and Force of Will for different restrictions and costs.

The collector version does not change that exchange. Use the live legality panel for your format instead of assuming a Commander deck reprint grants legality everywhere.

PRINTING HISTORY

From the debut to later physical editions

◇

Theros #65 was released on 27 September 2013. It is the debut, at rare, with Mohrbacher’s illustration and both ordinary finishes. Commander 2016 #98 later retained that artist but is a nonfoil-only deck printing.

Secret Lair #91, dated June 2020, introduced Allen Douglas’s illustration. The checked record is nonfoil-only. It is an artwork alternative, not evidence that every Secret Lair card has a matching foil.

Tales of Middle-earth Commander #197 followed in June 2023 with Axel Sauerwald’s art and nonfoil only. Keep the LTC product code separate from the main LTR set; a shared fictional setting does not make their collector records interchangeable.

Secret Lair #1591, dated February 2025, credits Kelogsloops and supports nonfoil and traditional foil. The table selects these contrasting physical editions rather than claiming to cover every later promotional release or every language.

ARTWORK & FRAME COMPARISON

Which differences change the illustration?

◇

THS #65 and C16 #98 make a useful same-artist comparison. A Commander reprint may suit a deck, but it is not the original Theros edition even though the central image is familiar.

Douglas, Sauerwald and Kelogsloops are separate credited artists, making those entries meaningful artwork comparisons. The inverted frame effect catalogued for #1591 is presentation metadata; it does not itself describe a new physical finish.

Compare the images without animation first. After choosing the illustration, check whether that exact collector number permits the reflective surface you want. Otherwise a listing labelled merely “alternate-art foil” can be misleading.

FOILS & SPECIAL TREATMENTS

Choose the printing before its finish

◇

Theros #65 provides both nonfoil and traditional foil. The C16 #98, SLD #91 and LTC #197 records in this shortlist are nonfoil-only. Kelogsloops’ SLD #1591 supplies another art choice with both finishes.

Do not infer surge foil from the LTC code or etched foil from a Secret Lair label. Those treatments need their own verified records. A catalogue scan and ManaScout’s digital shimmer cannot demonstrate a physical surface under room lighting.

MANASCOUT RECOMMENDATIONS

Which printing suits your collection?

◇

Original-art foil: THS #65 is the straightforward reference, with its original set identity and a genuine foil option.

Nonfoil artwork collection: compare Douglas’s #91 and Sauerwald’s LTC #197. Their finish limitation may be a benefit if you prefer consistent nonreflective deck cards.

Later art with a finish choice: SLD #1591 offers Kelogsloops’ illustration in either surface. Keep that number visible when checking availability.

Gameplay copy: the C16 reprint is a clear familiar-art option. Judge current offers on edition, language and condition, without treating this shortlist as a price ranking.
