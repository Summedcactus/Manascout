When was Mana Vault first printed?

◇

Mana Vault was first printed in Limited Edition Alpha in 1993. The original Alpha Mana Vault is collector number 259 and features artwork by Mark Tedin.

It was part of Magic from the game's earliest release and has since developed a very different printing history from many Alpha cards, receiving numerous later reprints and premium collector treatments.

MANASCOUT GUIDE

One mana in. Three mana out.

◇

Mana Vault is a one-mana artifact that can tap to produce three colourless mana.

That means a single mana can put Mana Vault onto the battlefield and potentially turn into three mana immediately, creating a large burst of acceleration extremely early in a game.

The drawback is that Mana Vault does not normally untap during its controller's untap step.

Instead, the card creates a tension between explosive acceleration now and the cost of getting the artifact ready to produce mana again later.

HOW IT WORKS

How does Mana Vault untap?

◇

Mana Vault does not untap during your normal untap step.

At the beginning of your upkeep, you may pay four mana. If you do, Mana Vault untaps.

If Mana Vault is still tapped at the beginning of your draw step, it deals one damage to you.

The card therefore excels at producing a burst of mana, but repeatedly using it without another way to untap artifacts comes with both a mana cost and a small life penalty.

FIRST PRINTING

The original Alpha Mana Vault

◇

The first Mana Vault appeared in Limited Edition Alpha in 1993.

The Alpha card is a rare, carries collector number 259 and features artwork by Mark Tedin.

Mana Vault then returned in Beta, Unlimited, Revised, Fourth Edition and Fifth Edition , meaning the original Mark Tedin artwork spans several eras of early Magic.

If you specifically want Mana Vault's first printing rather than simply an old printing, Alpha is the version to look for.

OLD-SCHOOL PRINTINGS

Alpha, Beta, Unlimited and early Mana Vaults

◇

Mana Vault has an unusually deep old-school printing history.

After Alpha, the card appeared in Beta and Unlimited, followed by Revised Edition and later core sets.

These versions preserve Mark Tedin's original artwork while changing details such as border colour, frame presentation, set identity and card wording.

That makes early Mana Vaults particularly interesting to compare: the artwork may be familiar across several releases, but the physical cards represent very different periods of Magic's history.

MANA VAULT REPRINTS

What changes between Mana Vault printings?

◇

Mana Vault has moved through several very different visual eras since Alpha.

Differences between printings can include artwork, frame style, border treatment, foil availability, extended art, borderless presentation and premium finish .

That means a Mana Vault can look like a piece of early 1990s Magic, a modern Masters-set mythic or an elaborate collector treatment while still representing the same famously explosive mana artifact.

KALADESH INVENTIONS

The Mana Vault Masterpiece printing

◇

One of Mana Vault's most distinctive premium versions appeared in Kaladesh Inventions in 2016.

The Invention printing is collector number 17, features artwork by Kirsten Zirngibl and was produced as a premium foil Masterpiece Series card.

Its ornate Kaladesh-inspired frame gives it a completely different visual identity from the original Mark Tedin card and from later Masters-set versions.

For collectors comparing high-end Mana Vault printings, the Kaladesh Invention is therefore one of the versions most visibly separated from a conventional Magic card frame.

ULTIMATE MASTERS

Mana Vault and the Ultimate Box Topper

◇

Ultimate Masters brought Mana Vault back in 2018 as a mythic rare.

That release uses artwork by Christine Choi, giving Mana Vault another major artistic identity after decades of printings using the original Mark Tedin illustration.

Mana Vault also received an Ultimate Box Topper version with an extended-art presentation.

For players who want a premium Mana Vault without using an early-edition frame or the ornate Kaladesh Invention style, the Ultimate Masters family offers a very different option.

DOUBLE MASTERS 2022

Mana Vault borderless and foil versions

◇

Mana Vault returned again in Double Masters 2022.

The set included a regular Mana Vault using the Christine Choi artwork as well as a borderless version with different artwork by Steve Prescott.

Different finishes expanded the choice further, giving players options across regular, foil, borderless and premium presentations.

The Double Masters 2022 borderless Mana Vault is particularly useful for collectors who want a modern full-art-style card without moving into the much older Alpha-era releases.

SECRET LAIR

The 2024 Secret Lair Mana Vault

◇

Mana Vault received another dramatic collector treatment through Secret Lair in 2024.

The Secret Lair printing is collector number 796★ and uses a borderless rainbow-foil presentation.

Its presentation is radically different from both the traditional early versions and the modern Masters-set cards, pushing Mana Vault firmly into showcase-piece territory.

It is a good example of how far Mana Vault's visual history has travelled since the original 1993 Alpha card.

ART & TREATMENTS

Mana Vault artwork, foils and premium versions

◇

Mana Vault now has several genuinely different artistic identities rather than simply a sequence of frame updates.

Mark Tedin defines the original and many early printings, Christine Choi provides the modern Masters-era interpretation, and other premium releases move even further away from the original visual language.

The Kaladesh Invention, Double Masters borderless card, Ultimate Box Topper and Secret Lair treatment each approach the same artifact from a very different collector angle.

That makes Mana Vault one of the stronger cards to compare visually rather than choosing solely by price.

WHICH ONE?

Which Mana Vault printing should you choose?

◇

For the original: choose Limited Edition Alpha from 1993.

For an old-school copy: compare Alpha, Beta, Unlimited, Revised and later early-frame releases depending on the period and border style you prefer.

For a deck copy: compare modern reprints and available prices rather than paying primarily for age or rarity.

For a Masterpiece: look at the 2016 Kaladesh Inventions version.

For modern borderless art: compare the Double Masters 2022 treatment.

For extended art: the Ultimate Masters Box Topper offers another premium interpretation.

For a true showcase piece: compare the 2024 Secret Lair borderless foil with Mana Vault's other premium versions.
