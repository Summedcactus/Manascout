RULES & FORMAT CONTEXT

Mystical Tutor in a deck

◇

Mystical Tutor searches for an instant or sorcery, reveals it and puts it on top after shuffling. It does not put that card directly into your hand. Enlightened Tutor and Worldly Tutor provide useful comparisons of the same Mirage-era collecting story while searching for different card types.

Legality reviewed 10 November 2026: Wizards lists Mystical Tutor as banned in Legacy and restricted in Vintage; the catalogue lists it legal in Commander. A modern reprint does not override those restrictions. The card’s availability as a collectible and its legality for your deck are separate questions.

PRINTING HISTORY

From the debut to later physical editions

◇

Mirage #80 is the original nonfoil uncommon. From the Vault: Exiled #8 followed in August 2009 with O’Connor’s image, mythic rarity and foil-only boxed presentation. The later mythic rarity does not rewrite the original card’s uncommon status.

Eternal Masters #62 introduced Lindsey Look’s illustration in June 2016 with both finishes. Signature Spellbook: Jace #6 arrived in June 2018 with Yongjae Choi’s artwork, a themed frame and nonfoil/foil options. A Magic Online promo using that picture is a platform record, not another physical Spellbook edition.

Secret Lair #334, dated November 2021, credits Mateus Manhanini and belongs to Far Out, Man. It supports nonfoil and foil. Dominaria Remastered then offers regular #60, retro #289 and borderless #421 in January 2023: Look appears on the first two, Ferguson on the borderless card.

Avatar: The Last Airbender Eternal #308 followed in November 2025 with Shane Beresford’s illustration and a nonfoil-only finish. TLE is a distinct Eternal product code; it should not be swapped for the main Avatar set code or described as a foil simply because a related product contains foils.

About this guide

Mystical Tutor first appeared in Mirage on 8 October 1996: MIR #80, an uncommon illustrated by David O’Connor, in nonfoil only. Its later catalogue spans six artist families, from an original-art boxed foil to Jace-themed, Secret Lair, retro-frame, borderless and Avatar editions. This guide identifies which changes are genuinely new paintings and which reuse an image inside a different frame or physical finish.

ARTWORK & FRAME COMPARISON

Which differences change the illustration?

◇

MIR #80 and V09 #8 are the original-art pair. Keep their edition labels visible: a From the Vault foil can use the original artist without becoming a Mirage foil.

EMA #62, DMR #60 and DMR #289 hold Look’s illustration broadly constant while changing product or frame. The retro DMR card is a 2023 reprint, not O’Connor’s original in a shiny finish. Ferguson’s DMR #421 supplies a different painting as well as borderless presentation.

Choi’s Spellbook card, Manhanini’s Secret Lair and Beresford’s Avatar card provide three further artistic directions. They are particularly useful alternatives if you collect a character, artist or setting rather than matching the oldest possible border across a deck.

FOILS & SPECIAL TREATMENTS

Choose the printing before its finish

◇

There is no Mirage booster foil for this card. V09 #8 is one verified original-art foil option. EMA #62, SS1 #6, SLD #334 and the three DMR records offer nonfoil and traditional foil.

The Avatar #308 is nonfoil-only in the checked record. Retro and borderless are printing/frame descriptions, not finishes you can select on every record. None of the listed DMR entries acquires an etched option merely because other premium Magic products use etched foil.

MANASCOUT RECOMMENDATIONS

Which printing suits your collection?

◇

First printing: choose MIR #80 for the Mirage identity and O’Connor artwork. Later uncommon-looking frames do not substitute for that original set.

Original art in foil: compare V09 #8 with a physical photograph of the actual boxed-product surface. A digital shimmer is not a finish sample.

Frame-led deck: compare DMR #60 and #289 in the same finish. For a new illustration, move to #421 rather than expecting the retro border to imply new art.

Character or artist theme: SS1 #6, SLD #334 and TLE #308 are distinct options with different finish availability. Use the explorer to inspect the precise set and collector number; the comparison does not claim a complete current stock list.
