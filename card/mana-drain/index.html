HOW IT WORKS

The mana arrives in your next main phase, not immediately

◇

Mana Drain costs {U}{U} and targets a spell. When it resolves, it attempts to counter that spell and creates a delayed trigger that adds colourless mana equal to the spell's mana value at the beginning of your next main phase. The amount is based on mana value, rather than simply the mana the opponent happened to spend.

That next main phase can be later in the same turn. If you cast Drain during your precombat main phase or combat phase, the delayed trigger occurs at the beginning of your postcombat main phase. If you use it during an opponent's turn, it will normally supply mana in your next precombat main phase.

If the target becomes illegal before Drain resolves, Drain does not resolve and supplies no delayed mana. If the target is legal but cannot be countered, the delayed mana trigger is still set up. These distinctions appear in Wizards' Thunder Junction release notes. They explain why Drain is more than a visually different Counterspell.

FIRST PRINTING & REPRINTS

A Legends original followed by several distinct returns

◇

The Legends printing establishes the original Mark Tedin illustration and early frame. It is an uncommon from 1994, even though later Masters and bonus-sheet versions carry a mythic symbol. Choosing a newer rarity symbol does not change the underlying spell or make a copy part of the original release.

Judge Gift Cards 2016 #2 supplies Mike Bierek's artwork in foil. Iconic Masters #65 followed in November 2017 with Raymond Swanland's illustration in nonfoil and traditional foil. These give a historical promo collector and a player seeking a later set copy different starting points.

Commander Legends in November 2020 prints Swanland's artwork on standard #80 and extended-art #637. Double Masters 2022 retains that artist on standard #57 and foil-etched #432, while borderless #348 changes the illustration to Chuck Lukacs. Set code and collector number are needed to separate those decisions.

The April 2024 Breaking News bonus sheet adds Florey's illustration at OTP #11, with a textured foil counterpart at #67. Its set code is OTP, not the main Outlaws of Thunder Junction set's OTJ. The bonus-sheet printing itself does not make the card legal in every format supported by the main set.

About this guide

Mana Drain turns an answer now into colourless mana in your next main phase. Its printing choices have a similarly distinct character: Mark Tedin's Legends original, Mike Bierek's Judge promo, Raymond Swanland's modern reprints, Chuck Lukacs' borderless interpretation and Florey's Breaking News treatment. The useful comparison is which combination of history, illustration and physical finish you want to own.

ART & TREATMENTS

Compare Tedin, Bierek, Swanland, Lukacs and Florey

◇

Tedin's Legends illustration belongs to Mana Drain's original visual identity. Bierek's Judge card is a separate interpretation with a promotional release behind it. If your collection is about a particular era or artist, those reasons may matter more than selecting the newest frame.

Swanland connects Iconic Masters, Commander Legends and the standard or etched Double Masters 2022 entries. CMR #80 against #637 is a useful way to compare the surrounding frame while keeping the artwork. Changing instead to Lukacs' 2X2 #348 changes the image as well as introducing borderless presentation.

Florey's Breaking News entry takes the card into a Breaking News showcase design. Compare OTP #11 with #67 if that illustration is already your preference and the remaining question is the physical finish. Wizards explains the bonus-sheet presentation and textured treatment in Collecting Outlaws of Thunder Junction.

That separation of decisions is useful here: extended art is not automatically new art, and a new artist is not automatically a new finish. The linked printing records make the artist, set and number visible before you move to current prices.

FOILS & PREMIUM FINISHES

Legends has no foil; etched and textured belong to later releases

◇

There is no official foil edition of LEG #65. J16 #2 is foil only, while the selected IMA and standard CMR entries offer nonfoil or traditional foil. CMR #637 also has both choices, so extended art alone does not identify a reflective copy.

For Double Masters 2022, standard #57 and borderless #348 each come in nonfoil and traditional foil. Etched #432 is its own entry, uses Swanland's art and has only the etched finish. It should not be described as an etched version of Lukacs' borderless illustration.

OTP #11 offers nonfoil and traditional foil; OTP #67 is textured foil only. Wizards identifies textured Breaking News mythics as Collector Booster treatments. Confirm the number and finish together instead of treating textured and etched as interchangeable words.

ManaScout's shimmer is illustrative. It cannot reproduce the texture of the Breaking News finish, establish a Judge card's authenticity or reveal the condition of a seller's copy. Use photographs when deciding whether a physical treatment merits the extra spending.

WHICH ONE?

Which Mana Drain printing should you choose?

◇

For the original: choose LEG #65 and check the edition, language and condition. Later mythic copies supply the same game function but a different historical object.

For a playing budget: compare nonfoil IMA #65, CMR #80, 2X2 #57 and OTP #11 using current prices and comparable condition. Do not assume the borderless or older copy must be more expensive in every listing; compare the actual available options.

For Swanland's artwork: start with the standard entries, then compare extended CMR #637 and etched 2X2 #432. If you want another illustration, add Lukacs' #348 before deciding whether to choose a foil.

For a promo or finish-focused collection: Bierek's J16 #2 and Florey's textured OTP #67 offer different reasons to spend beyond a basic deck copy. The former is an artist-and-promo choice; the latter keeps the Breaking News identity and changes its surface.
