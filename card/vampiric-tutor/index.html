HOW IT WORKS

A selected card on top, with life loss during resolution

◇

Vampiric Tutor is an instant costing {B}. It searches for a card, shuffles and puts that card on top of your library, then loses you two life. The two life are part of the spell's effect, rather than an additional casting cost.

The chosen card is not put into your hand by the Tutor. You still need a draw or another way to access the top card. That makes timing relevant: using an instant-speed Tutor before your next draw is a different resource exchange from casting Demonic Tutor to put a card directly into your hand.

The shuffle-and-put-on-top instruction is handled together. The source's ruling explains that if the top of your library is face up, the second card down is not exposed during that action. Use the live rules text as the common reference when comparing older terminology with modern printings.

FIRST PRINTING & REPRINTS

Visions history, a Judge illustration and the Swanland reprints

◇

The original Leach illustration and Visions set identity define VIS #72. It is the historical debut, not a foil option waiting to be selected. A later release can reuse familiar card text while representing a different product and physical finish.

Eternal Masters #112 in June 2016 uses Raymond Swanland's artwork and is mythic, with nonfoil and traditional foil options. The 2018 Judge promo, J18 #2, instead credits Lucas Graciano and is foil only. Those are distinct art choices as well as different release histories.

Commander Legends #656 in November 2020 offers Swanland's illustration in extended art. Dominaria Remastered in January 2023 supplies his standard #108 and retro-frame #314, plus Ferguson's borderless #430. A retro frame is therefore not a return to Leach's original Visions image.

The 2026 Secrets of Strixhaven Mystical Archive adds SOA #35 by Paul Adam. The shortlist below concentrates on these artist and frame comparisons; the live archive also contains further language and treatment records.

About this guide

Vampiric Tutor offers a precise card on top of your library for one black mana and two life, rather than putting that card straight into your hand. Its collector choices are equally specific: Gary Leach's Visions original, Lucas Graciano's Judge foil, Raymond Swanland's reprints or later Archive and borderless illustrations. Here is how to identify the version behind each description.

ART & TREATMENTS

Separate the Visions image from a modern retro frame

◇

If you want the card as it originally appeared, Leach's VIS #72 is the reference. DMR #314 has retro styling but uses Swanland's illustration. These choices can suit different collectors even when both are intended for a deck with older-looking cards.

Swanland connects EMA #112, extended-art CMR #656 and the DMR standard and retro entries. Comparing those versions keeps the illustration fixed while you judge the frame. Switching to Ferguson's #430 changes the artist as well as the borderless layout.

Graciano's J18 #2 offers another illustration in a Judge release. Adam's SOA #35 places the card in the later Archive frame. Wizards shows the DMR variants and explains the new Archive presentation in Collecting Secrets of Strixhaven.

FOILS & PREMIUM FINISHES

The foil choice begins with a later edition

◇

There is no official foil VIS #72. For a foil from the selected releases, compare Graciano's foil-only J18 #2 with traditional foil copies of the Swanland or Ferguson entries. The artist should be part of that comparison rather than an accidental change.

CMR #656 and the three selected DMR versions each offer nonfoil and traditional foil. Extended art, retro and borderless therefore describe presentation, not an automatic foil finish. SOA #35 likewise has nonfoil and traditional foil choices.

The explorer may contain further premium or Japanese Archive records. Check their individual set, number, language and finish rather than assuming that every Archive card has the same availability. The shimmer is illustrative; photographs are still needed to judge an actual foil surface and condition.

WHICH ONE?

Which Vampiric Tutor printing should you choose?

◇

For the debut: choose VIS #72. Its interest is the Visions release and Leach artwork, rather than a foil treatment.

For a playing budget: compare available nonfoil EMA #112, DMR #108 and other standard entries in similar condition. Use current figures rather than assuming the newest or oldest copy will always be cheapest.

For Swanland's image: compare CMR #656 with DMR #108 and #314 to decide between extended art, standard and retro frames. Then choose nonfoil or traditional foil within the entry you prefer.

For another artist: Graciano's J18 #2 is a foil-only promotional choice; Ferguson's DMR #430 and Adam's SOA #35 allow a nonfoil option too. That difference can matter when personalising a nonfoil deck.

◇ MANASCOUT PERSPECTIVE

Where to start comparing Vampiric Tutor

◇

Compare the three Swanland frame options first if you already like his illustration. If the frame is not the main attraction, put the Visions original, Judge foil and Ferguson borderless image beside it. That makes the extra spending a decision about a specific artist or object rather than an unspecified premium tier.
