MTG PRINTING GUIDE

Arcane Signet

Arcane Signet began as a Brawl-deck card, yet its printing catalogue now offers far more than the original crystal. A useful collector comparison separates Dan Murayama Scott's artwork from Dani Pendergast's Secret Lair interpretation, Drew Tucker's borderless version and unusual frame or foil choices. Here are the releases behind those differences, without treating every Commander reprint as a new illustration.

QUICK ANSWER

When was Arcane Signet first printed?

◇

Arcane Signet debuted with Throne of Eldraine in 2019, whose release date was 4 October. The original ELD #331 is a common illustrated by Dan Murayama Scott. It was included in the Brawl decks, rather than the regular Draft Booster card pool, and this printing is nonfoil only.

Collector shortcut: CMR #689 extends the original artwork, SLD #201 changes the artist to Dani Pendergast, CMM #653 is Drew Tucker's borderless version, and MB2 #254 uses the Future Sight frame in traditional foil.

HOW IT WORKS

Your commander's identity supplies the available colours

◇

Arcane Signet costs {2} and taps to add one mana of a colour in your commander's colour identity. With two commanders, their combined identities determine the options. The identity is established before the game; it does not change because a commander moves to a hidden zone or changes colour during play.

A colourless commander's identity does not let the Signet produce {C}: the ability produces no mana when that identity contains no colours. It also produces no mana in a game without a commander. Wizards states these limits in the Throne of Eldraine release notes.

That makes it a different tool from Sol Ring. Signet supplies one appropriate colour, while Sol Ring produces two colourless mana. Choosing elaborate artwork does not change either card's role or make Signet a source of unrestricted colours.

FIRST PRINTING & REPRINTS

A Brawl original, followed by Commander printings

◇

ELD #331 belongs to the original Brawl release. Wizards' Brawl-deck feature lists it in those decks. The ELD set code therefore does not mean that it was an ordinary card in the set's Draft Boosters; the product context is part of identifying its debut.

Commander Legends in November 2020 added standard #297 and extended-art #689, both credited to Dan Murayama Scott and both offering nonfoil and traditional foil. These make a useful pair when you like the original illustration but want to compare frame presentation or add a foil.

Secret Lair's A Box of Rocks supplied #201, with Dani Pendergast's artwork, in 2020. Commander Masters #653 followed in August 2023 with Drew Tucker's borderless illustration. Those releases change the artistic interpretation, whereas moving between the two Commander Legends numbers keeps the artist and image.

ART & TREATMENTS

Changing the frame is different from changing the artist

◇

If Scott's original crystal is the attraction, start by comparing ELD #331, CMR #297 and CMR #689. The extended-art version changes how the illustration fills the card. A later set symbol by itself does not imply new artwork.

Pendergast's SLD #201 and Tucker's CMM #653 provide separate artistic directions. The Secretversary announcement identifies A Box of Rocks as an alternative-art mana-rock collection; #201 is the number to use when distinguishing this Signet from other Secret Lair versions.

Mystery Booster 2 #254 reuses Scott's art inside the Future Sight frame. It was among that release's foil-only Future Sight entries, as shown in Wizards' product breakdown. It is a later reprint of a 2019 card, not evidence that Arcane Signet appeared in the 2007 Future Sight set.

Fallout #752 adds Eddie Mendoza's artwork with a surge finish. If you prefer a setting-specific deck, that gives the comparison a different purpose from simply widening the original crystal's frame. Check the exact PIP entry rather than assuming that every Fallout Signet shares its number or finish.

FOILS & PREMIUM FINISHES

An original Signet and a foil Signet are different purchases

◇

The original ELD #331 is nonfoil. For a foil with Scott's artwork, the standard or extended-art Commander Legends entries are concrete alternatives; they are later releases with their own set and collector numbers.

SLD #201 and CMM #653 each offer a nonfoil choice as well as a foil choice. A listing described as Secret Lair or borderless is therefore incomplete without its finish. For an unusual frame in the 2024 Mystery Booster 2 release, #254 is traditional foil only.

PIP #752 is surge foil only. Wizards' Fallout collecting guide explains the product's surge treatment. The catalogue and seller photographs should identify the physical finish; ManaScout's shimmer is an illustration, not a reproduction of surge foil's surface pattern.

WHICH ONE?

Which Arcane Signet printing should you choose?

◇

For the debut: choose ELD #331 and accept its nonfoil finish. Its interest is the original Brawl release, rather than a unique gameplay ability.

For the familiar art in a deck: compare standard Commander reprints and CMR #297. If you specifically want a wider presentation of that image, add #689 to the comparison and select the finish separately.

For an artist-led collection: put Pendergast's SLD #201 beside Tucker's CMM #653. Both change the illustration, making this a more meaningful art decision than a set-symbol swap.

For unusual frames or finishes: MB2 #254 suits a Future Sight-frame theme, while Fallout #752 suits a crossover or surge-foil collection. Use current prices and comparable condition; no version is assumed to stay cheapest.

◇ MANASCOUT PERSPECTIVE

Where to start comparing Arcane Signet

◇

Compare the Scott standard and extended-art versions first to decide whether you want the same image with a different frame. Then add Pendergast and Tucker if the illustration itself is what you want to change. This separates an artwork preference from a preference for a more elaborate object.
