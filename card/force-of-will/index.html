HOW IT WORKS

No normal mana payment still means a real cost

◇

Force of Will normally costs {3}{U}{U} and counters a target spell. Instead of paying that mana cost, you may pay one life and exile a blue card from your hand. This lets you interact while your lands are tapped, but it uses an additional card and a point of life rather than making the exchange resource-free.

The alternative payment does not change its mana value, which remains five. Cost increases can still apply. Wizards explains that calculation in the Dominaria Remastered release notes, so a tax effect can matter even when you choose the life-and-card option.

Its target is a spell on the stack, not an ability or an already-resolved permanent. That distinguishes it from Deflecting Swat, which can redirect the targets of a spell or ability. Every printing here represents the same Force of Will; rarity and foil treatment do not change how its alternative cost works.

FIRST PRINTING & REPRINTS

An Alliances uncommon with several later collector identities

◇

The original ALL #28 ties Force of Will to Alliances and Nielsen's artwork. Its uncommon rarity is part of that release's history. Later mythic printings are separate editions, so a rarity symbol is not a reliable shortcut for identifying the original.

The 2014 Judge edition, J14 #4, credits Matt Stewart and is foil only. Eternal Masters #49 followed in June 2016 with Nielsen credited again, this time in a release offering both nonfoil and foil. That gives collectors distinct historical points to compare without assuming every early-looking or Nielsen-credited copy is an Alliances card.

Amonkhet Invocations #14 in 2017 introduced Jaime Jones' illustration within the Invocation presentation. Double Masters #340 in 2020 added Scott M. Fischer's borderless box-topper interpretation. The artist and frame both matter when distinguishing these premium releases.

Dominaria Remastered in January 2023 offers an especially clear comparison: standard #50 and retro #284 use Donato Giancola's art, while borderless #418 credits Richard Kane Ferguson. The 2026 Secrets of Strixhaven Mystical Archive adds further artwork under the SOA set code.

About this guide

Force of Will lets a blue player trade a card and a life for an answer without paying its normal five-mana cost. Its collecting history offers a different set of trade-offs: an Alliances original, an early Judge foil, a dramatically styled Invocation or a later illustration in a retro or borderless frame. Start with the exact edition, then decide which artwork and finish deserve the place in your deck or collection.

ART & TREATMENTS

Retro styling does not mean original artwork

◇

If your goal is the Alliances illustration in its original setting, ALL #28 is the reference. DMR #284 may look appropriate beside older cards, but it is a 2023 retro-frame printing with Giancola's art. The old-frame aesthetic and the historical original are two different collecting choices.

The DMR pair #50 and #284 lets you compare the frame while keeping Giancola's illustration. Switching to #418 changes the artist to Ferguson as well as the borderless presentation. Wizards shows the treatments in the Dominaria Remastered variant gallery.

Stewart's Judge foil, Jones' Invocation and Fischer's box topper provide other artist-led choices. Treat them as individual interpretations rather than a single premium tier. The Invocation frame in particular is a presentation decision as well as an illustration decision; compare the complete card image and text layout.

For the 2026 Archive, Rovina Cai's SOA #19 and Hatori Kyoka's Japanese #84 are different artwork families. Kyoka's silver scroll #149 shares the Japanese-art direction but changes the finish. The Japanese text is part of that choice, not merely a field to overlook when ordering.

FOILS & PREMIUM FINISHES

Judge foil, Invocation and silver scroll are different objects

◇

ALL #28 has no official foil edition. J14 #4 and MP2 #14 are foil-only entries with different artists and presentations. A listing described simply as a foil Force of Will therefore needs its set and number before it can be compared sensibly.

The selected EMA, Double Masters and DMR entries offer nonfoil and traditional foil. Borderless does not itself mean foil, and a retro-frame foil should not be represented as an original Alliances foil. Decide which image and frame you want, then compare the finishes within that entry.

SOA #84 is the Japanese nonfoil entry; #149 is the silver scroll foil. Wizards' Secrets of Strixhaven collecting guide explains the Japanese artwork and Collector Booster silver scroll treatment. Keep the SOA code distinct from the earlier STA Mystical Archive release.

The shared shimmer illustrates a foil preview. Photographs of an offered copy are still needed to assess the physical finish, surface and condition; an animated image cannot recreate an Invocation or silver scroll surface.

WHICH ONE?

Which Force of Will printing should you choose?

◇

For the historical original: choose ALL #28 and check the physical edition and condition. A later frame with older styling does not substitute for the original release if that history is your collecting goal.

For a defined playing budget: compare available nonfoils such as ALL #28, EMA #49 and DMR #50 in comparable condition. Use the current figures rather than assuming a permanent price order, especially if buying multiple copies.

For a retro-frame foil deck: DMR #284 is a specific option. For a borderless illustration, compare Fischer's 2XM #340 with Ferguson's DMR #418 before deciding whether to pay for a foil finish.

For a promotional or display collection: Stewart's J14 #4 and Jones' MP2 #14 offer distinct visual identities. For Japanese artwork, compare SOA #84 and silver scroll #149 with the language and finish recorded explicitly.
