MTG PRINTING GUIDE

The One Ring

The One Ring debuted on 23 June 2023 in The Lord of the Rings: Tales of Middle-earth. The original standard record is LTR #246, a mythic rare illustrated by Veli Nystrom, in nonfoil and traditional foil. Six useful collecting families organise the versions: standard/extended art, the Mount Doom scene, scroll presentation, poster art, the unique 001/001 object and the later Hobbit artwork. The same card name does not mean that every copy is serialized, surge foil or part of the same illustration.

QUICK ANSWER

When was The One Ring first printed?

◇

The One Ring debuted in The Lord of the Rings: Tales of Middle-earth, released on 23 June 2023. The original standard printing is LTR #246, a mythic rare illustrated by Veli Nystrom, available in nonfoil and traditional foil.

Collector shortcut: LTR #451 is Marta Nael's borderless scene version; #697 is the Scrolls of Middle-earth treatment; #748 is Justine Jones' poster art. Extended-art surge foil #791 and serialized poster #748z are separate premium records. Neither is the unique 001/001 Ring.

Identification shortcut: LTR #246/#380 compare standard and extended presentation of Nystrom’s art; #451 is Marta Nael’s scene; #697 is scroll style; #748 is Justine Jones’ poster. Serialized #748z, surge #791 and the unique catalogue #0 are distinct premium records, not universal finish options.

PRINTING IDENTIFICATION

Which The One Ring printing is this?

◇

Ordinary versus expanded original art: LTR #246 is standard-frame and #380 is extended art. Both credit Nystrom and have nonfoil/foil records. “Full art” without a number does not tell you whether this is the original image or a scene/poster illustration.

Scene or scroll? LTR #451 credits Marta Nael and forms part of the Mount Doom scene. Scroll #697 retains Nystrom’s credit with its own frame and silver-foil product treatment. They are not interchangeable alternate-art labels.

Poster or serialized poster? #748 and #748z credit Justine Jones. The regular poster supports nonfoil/traditional foil; the serialized record is double rainbow foil. Inspect the physical serial, not just the poster artwork.

The one-of-one or a later reprint? Catalogue #0 is the unique 001/001 Ring, not the later poster serial. HOC #44/#84 instead credit Dan Frazier for The Hobbit Eternal: ordinary nonfoil/foil and a separate surge-foil record.

HOW IT WORKS

Card draw that accumulates a burden

◇

The One Ring is a four-mana legendary artifact with indestructible. If you cast it, its enters-the-battlefield ability gives you protection from everything until your next turn. That protection applies to you, rather than granting the same protection to every permanent you control.

Tapping the Ring puts a burden counter on it, then draws a card for each burden counter it has. At the beginning of your upkeep, you lose one life for each burden counter on it. Activating it repeatedly increases both its draw output and the potential upkeep payment in life.

Protection does not stop every possible way to lose life or affect a player, and indestructible does not stop exile or a return-to-hand effect. The official release notes explain the protection interactions. Check current format legality in the live details before choosing a copy for a particular deck.

FIRST PRINTING & REPRINTS

The June debut and November collector expansion

◇

The standard LTR #246 and extended-art #380 share Veli Nystrom's artwork. The extended-art treatment changes the presentation of that illustration; it should not be confused with the separate borderless scene painting by Marta Nael on #451.

Nael's Ring forms part of the Mount Doom scene with Frodo, Sauron's Bane; Samwise the Stouthearted; and Gollum, Patient Plotter. The Bundle and Gift Bundle supplied the scene cards in traditional foil, while nonfoil versions were available in Collector Boosters. That gives #451 a story-display purpose beyond being an alternative deck copy.

The November 2023 Special Edition release added Scrolls of Middle-earth #697, poster #748 and extended-art surge foil #791. These belong to the expanded LTR release rather than a different underlying card. The later Hobbit Eternal reprints, HOC #44 and #84 from August 2026, add Dan Frazier's artwork to the comparison.

ART & TREATMENTS

Scene, scroll, poster or a different artist?

◇

Start with the distinction between artwork and frame. LTR #246 and #380 use the same Nystrom illustration in standard and extended-art presentations. Scrolls #697 also credits Nystrom, but its showcase layout gives the text and name a different surrounding design.

For a genuinely different illustration, compare Nael's #451 scene card with Jones' #748 poster. The scene invites you to assemble a particular story moment across four cards. The poster treatment makes the individual card a much more graphic display piece. Neither term specifies a foil finish on its own.

The 2026 HOC #44 and surge foil #84 introduce Dan Frazier's interpretation. Compare those with the LTR images if choosing by artist, and keep the set code in any saved note: a bare collector number cannot reliably identify a card across releases.

FOILS & PREMIUM FINISHES

Four foil families and two very different serializations

◇

Traditional foil versions exist for standard #246, extended-art #380, scene #451 and poster #748. Scrolls #697 instead pairs nonfoil with silver foil. The surge extended-art version is #791, and the Hobbit surge version is HOC #84. Those numbers are useful safeguards when comparing listings described only as a premium Ring.

The unique 001/001 edition is catalogued as LTR #0. It uses Black Speech in Tengwar letterforms and traditional foil. Wizards confirms it has already been found. It is a historical collectible, not an edition that remains available to open in new packs.

The November serialized poster edition is a different release: LTR #748z in the catalogue, numbered out of 100 and using double rainbow foil. The suffix identifies the database record; the physical serial stamp identifies an individual card. Do not confuse a serialized poster with the one-of-one Ring or with a nonserialized #748 foil.

Wizards' Tales of Middle-earth collecting guide explains the product treatments. ManaScout's shimmer is illustrative; photographs are still needed to judge the actual finish, surface and any serial stamp.

WHICH ONE?

Which The One Ring printing should you choose?

◇

For the original appearance: choose LTR #246. Compare #380 if you like the same illustration with extended art, rather than assuming it is a different painting.

For a deck copy: compare ordinary nonfoils and scene #451 in similar condition, using current prices and your format's legality. The Bundle scene version is a real printing to compare, not shorthand for every foil Ring.

For a Tolkien display: #451 is the natural starting point if assembling the Mount Doom scene. For an individual graphic piece, compare Jones' poster #748; for another artist's interpretation, compare Frazier's HOC #44.

For a particular finish: use #697 for silver foil, #791 or HOC #84 for surge, and #748z for the serialized double rainbow poster. Verify the exact set, number and finish rather than relying on the card name or an animated preview.

REVIEWED COLLECTOR NOTES

A practical comparison path

◇

The first decision is whether you are comparing an edition or a different illustration. Nystrom’s regular #246 and extended-art #380 are an edition-and-frame pair. Nael’s scene #451 changes the image. Jones’ poster #748 changes the artistic direction again. These are not four finishes of a single printing, even though all use the same Oracle card.

Treat the serialized records as separate objects in a collection. LTR #0 is the singular serialized One Ring printing; #748z belongs to the later serialized poster family. An ordinary #748 poster foil is not made serialized by looking similar in a photograph. Record the complete collector number and inspect the physical serial stamp where applicable.

The later Hobbit Eternal entries add Dan Frazier’s artwork, with #44 offering nonfoil and traditional foil and #84 reserved for surge foil. This extends the guide beyond the 2023 launch without replacing its scene, scroll and poster history. HOC is separate from LTR, just as the related Sol Ring is a mechanically separate artifact.

Legality reviewed 10 November 2026: Wizards’ current Modern banned list includes The One Ring. Its Commander collectible and gameplay context must not be copied into a Modern recommendation. Choosing the ordinary #246 rather than a premium version does not change a ban. Always check the live format record before building.

COLLECTOR COMPARISON

Choose artwork, edition and finish separately

◇

Start a comparison by holding either artwork or finish constant. LTR #246 versus #380 asks about framing around the original illustration. #380 versus #791 asks about ordinary extended art and the later surge treatment. #246 versus #451 or #748 asks about different paintings and compositions.

Exceptionally scarce editions need their own labels. The unique Ring and serialized poster belong to different products and catalogue identities. Buying an ordinary foil does not buy serialization, and a card shown with a dramatic digital shimmer has not thereby been verified as surge, silver or double rainbow foil.

The later HOC pair is worth keeping in the comparison because it offers Frazier’s new artwork without erasing the 2023 families. For another Tolkien-related collector story, compare Sol Ring’s Quenya Rings of Power. They are mechanically Sol Ring, not copies of this legendary artifact. Modern legality remains a separate check: Wizards’ current banned list includes The One Ring, irrespective of the printing you choose.
