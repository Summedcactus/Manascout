When was Esper Sentinel first printed?

◇

Esper Sentinel was first printed in Modern Horizons 2 in 2021. The original regular-frame printing is collector number 12 and features artwork by Eric Deschamps.

Modern Horizons 2 also gave Esper Sentinel a sketch showcase treatment, meaning the card began its printing history with more than one visual presentation rather than waiting years for its first alternative version.

MANASCOUT GUIDE

White card draw disguised as a one-mana tax

◇

Esper Sentinel is a one-mana Artifact Creature — Human Soldier with a 1/1 body and a deceptively powerful triggered ability.

Whenever an opponent casts their first noncreature spell each turn, Esper Sentinel allows you to draw a card unless that player pays an additional amount of mana equal to Esper Sentinel's power.

That gives opponents a choice: spend additional mana to stop the draw, or let the Esper Sentinel player gain card advantage.

In multiplayer Commander that pressure can apply separately to several opponents, which is why such a small creature can have an outsized effect on a table.

HOW IT WORKS

How does Esper Sentinel's tax work?

◇

Esper Sentinel triggers when an opponent casts their first noncreature spell of the turn.

When the ability resolves, that player may pay X, where X is Esper Sentinel's power. If they do not pay, Esper Sentinel's controller draws a card.

Importantly, the ability checks Esper Sentinel's power when the trigger resolves, not when it first goes onto the stack.

Increasing Esper Sentinel's power can therefore make the tax harder to pay. If Esper Sentinel has left the battlefield before the trigger resolves, the game uses the power it had immediately before leaving.

COMMANDER STAPLE

Why is Esper Sentinel so strong in Commander?

◇

Multiplayer games make Esper Sentinel's trigger much more threatening than it might first appear.

Each opponent can trigger it with their first noncreature spell on their own turn, meaning one inexpensive creature can potentially create several separate opportunities to draw.

Even when opponents choose to pay, Esper Sentinel can force them to use mana less efficiently.

That combination of early deployment, card advantage and taxation explains why the card became strongly associated with Commander so soon after its release.

FIRST PRINTING

The original Modern Horizons 2 Esper Sentinel

◇

Esper Sentinel first appeared in Modern Horizons 2 in 2021.

The standard version is a rare with collector number 12 and artwork by Eric Deschamps.

If you specifically want Esper Sentinel's first conventional printing, Modern Horizons 2 #12 is therefore the version to look for.

Unlike many older staples, Esper Sentinel does not need decades of history to have a varied printing catalogue: the original release itself already included a radically different showcase treatment.

SKETCH SHOWCASE

Esper Sentinel sketch art from Modern Horizons 2

◇

Modern Horizons 2 included a sketch showcase Esper Sentinel as collector number 328.

The treatment uses the same Eric Deschamps concept but presents the card through a design-sketch aesthetic rather than the conventional finished-card layout.

One of the most distinctive details is the visible creative direction text in the card's presentation, making it feel closer to a piece of production artwork than a normal Magic printing.

Both foil and non-foil versions exist, giving collectors a choice even within the sketch treatment itself.

RETRO FRAME

Esper Sentinel retro-frame printing

◇

Esper Sentinel later received a retro-frame treatment, giving a card originally designed in 2021 the appearance of a much older Magic release.

The retro-frame version is collector number 2 in the Modern Horizons retro reprint group and retains Eric Deschamps' artwork.

It is available in both foil and non-foil versions.

The treatment creates one of Esper Sentinel's strongest visual contrasts: the card's modern tax-and-draw design presented in an old-frame aesthetic that predates the card by decades.

SECRET LAIR

Esper Sentinel Secret Lair artwork

◇

Esper Sentinel has now also entered Secret Lair territory.

In 2026, Wizards revealed a new Esper Sentinel for the Secret Lair Commander Deck: Odds and Ends .

The deck centres on artwork by Ricardo Cavolo, and Esper Sentinel is among the specially illustrated foil cards receiving new artwork created for the release.

Cavolo's treatment pushes Esper Sentinel much further away from the original Modern Horizons 2 look and gives collectors another genuinely different artistic interpretation rather than simply another set-symbol change.

ESPER SENTINEL REPRINTS

What changes between Esper Sentinel printings?

◇

Esper Sentinel has fewer years of printing history than cards such as Sol Ring or Lightning Bolt, but its versions already cover several dramatically different styles.

Differences can include frame treatment, showcase design, artwork, foil finish, set identity and premium presentation .

The original Modern Horizons 2 version offers the standard look, the sketch treatment exposes the card's concept-art aesthetic, the retro frame gives it an old-school identity, and Secret Lair provides completely new artwork.

That makes Esper Sentinel particularly well suited to direct printing comparison despite being a relatively modern card.

ART & TREATMENTS

Esper Sentinel artwork, retro frames and foils

◇

Esper Sentinel's small number of major visual treatments actually makes the differences between them easier to see.

The original Eric Deschamps card has the clean Modern Horizons 2 presentation, while the sketch showcase deliberately exposes the concept stage of the artwork.

The retro-frame printing applies a much older Magic visual language to the same card, while the Secret Lair version introduces an entirely different artistic interpretation.

For players who care about personalising Commander staples, that gives Esper Sentinel several versions that feel genuinely different rather than interchangeable.

WHICH ONE?

Which Esper Sentinel printing should you choose?
