HOW IT WORKS

A draw trigger that rewards increasing its power

◇

Esper Sentinel is a {W} 1/1 artifact creature. It triggers when an opponent casts their first noncreature spell each turn. Unless that opponent pays mana equal to Sentinel's power, you draw a card. Each opponent is tracked separately, and the first-spell check starts again each turn.

The power is checked when the ability resolves. Increasing Sentinel's power before resolution raises the payment; if it has left the battlefield, the ability uses its last known power. If an opponent already cast a noncreature spell that turn before Sentinel arrived, a later spell from that opponent does not qualify.

This gives Sentinel a distinct identity beside Mystic Remora: it can benefit from creature buffs, but it does not keep triggering on every noncreature spell from the same opponent that turn. Wizards confirms these interactions in the Modern Horizons 2 release notes.

FIRST PRINTING & REPRINTS

The sketch was part of the debut; the retro frame was not

◇

Modern Horizons 2 #12 establishes the original finished-art appearance. Its companion #328 uses the showcase sketch treatment and carries a piece of the creative direction in the flavour-text area. Eric Deschamps is credited on both.

The List later reproduced the original presentation as PLST #MH2-12 in nonfoil. The small Planeswalker symbol distinguishes a List copy from the initial set printing. A separate prerelease record, PMH2 #12s, is foil; check the stamp and catalogue entry when comparing an advertised promo.

The retro-frame H2R #2 arrived on 14 June 2024 through Modern Horizons 3 Collector Boosters. The catalogue calls this group Modern Horizons 2 Timeshifts. H2R is the code to search even though the distribution product is Modern Horizons 3. Wizards documents that grouping in Collecting Modern Horizons 3.

About this guide

Esper Sentinel can look like a finished illustration, an artist's working sketch or a card from an earlier era of Magic. Those choices began with Modern Horizons 2 in 2021; the retro frame and Ricardo Cavolo Secret Lair art came later. This guide separates the releases so you can choose a specific version of white's one-mana tax-and-draw creature.

ART & TREATMENTS

Finished art, sketch, retro or Ricardo Cavolo?

◇

Choose MH2 #12 if you want the image as it originally appeared in a conventional Magic frame. Choose #328 if the creative process is the attraction: the exposed sketch treatment and art-direction text make the presentation visibly different even though the artist is the same.

H2R #2 retains Deschamps' finished illustration in a retro frame. It is a styling option for a modern card, rather than evidence that Sentinel existed in the era the frame evokes. This is an especially useful comparison for a deck whose other cards already use retro frames.

Secret Lair #2123 changes the artist and overall visual approach. Ricardo Cavolo created the illustration and text layout for the Odds and Ends treatment, one of that deck's specially illustrated foil cards. The official deck reveal shows Sentinel alongside the other Cavolo cards, letting you assess the style as a group.

FOILS & PREMIUM FINISHES

Foil options without guessing from the frame

◇

The standard MH2 #12, sketch #328 and retro H2R #2 each offer nonfoil and traditional foil choices. A sketch or retro listing therefore does not, by itself, identify the finish. Confirm both the collector number and the stated finish.

The List #MH2-12 is nonfoil, while prerelease #12s and Cavolo's SLD #2123 are foil-only catalogue entries. Those distinctions help avoid treating every reprint or promotional version as interchangeable.

WHICH ONE?

Which Esper Sentinel printing should you choose?

◇

For the first conventional appearance: choose MH2 #12. If collecting the debut release rather than only its standard frame, #328 belongs in that comparison too.

For a deck copy with the original art: compare MH2 #12 with The List #MH2-12 in similar condition. A changed release label does not change how Sentinel plays.

For something about the making of Magic: sketch #328 gives you the development-art aesthetic and creative-direction text. Compare it in nonfoil and foil before deciding which is easier for you to read.

For an older-frame theme: use H2R #2. For a wholly different artist's interpretation, compare Cavolo's foil-only #2123 rather than paying for another frame around the original illustration.

◇ MANASCOUT PERSPECTIVE

Where to start comparing Esper Sentinel

◇

Compare #12 and #328 side by side first. The sketch changes the character of the card more than a set-symbol swap, and it lets you decide whether your collection is about the finished image or the creative work behind it. H2R #2 is the next comparison if matching your deck's frames matters most.
