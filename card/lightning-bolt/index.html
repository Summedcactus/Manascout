When was Lightning Bolt first printed?

◇

Lightning Bolt was first printed in Limited Edition Alpha in 1993. The original card is Alpha collector number 161 and features artwork by Christopher Rush.

Bolt was therefore part of Magic from the very beginning, before becoming one of the game's most recognisable red spells and receiving decades of later printings.

MANASCOUT GUIDE

One red mana. Three damage. More than three decades of Magic.

◇

Lightning Bolt is a one-mana red instant that deals three damage to any target.

Few Magic cards communicate their purpose as cleanly. For a single red mana, Bolt can remove a creature, finish a player or planeswalker, or simply turn spare mana into immediate damage.

Its efficiency helped make one mana for three damage one of the most recognisable benchmarks in Magic's history.

That history is reflected in its printings. Lightning Bolt can be collected as a piece of early Magic, played as an inexpensive modern reprint or chosen specifically for one of its many alternative-art and promotional versions.

THE BENCHMARK

Why is Lightning Bolt so iconic?

◇

Lightning Bolt does something extremely simple at an exceptionally efficient rate: three damage for one red mana.

It can interact with creatures while also contributing directly towards ending the game, meaning the same card can perform different jobs depending on the board state.

The result is a spell whose name has effectively become shorthand for efficient red direct damage.

That recognisability also gives Lightning Bolt unusually broad collector appeal: players may want the cheapest copy available, their favourite artwork, an early printing or a premium version of a card they have cast hundreds of times.

FIRST PRINTING

The original Alpha Lightning Bolt

◇

The first Lightning Bolt printing appeared in Limited Edition Alpha in 1993.

The Alpha card is numbered 161 and uses the original Lightning Bolt artwork by Christopher Rush.

Lightning Bolt then appeared in other early editions including Beta, Unlimited, Revised and Fourth Edition , giving the same fundamental card several distinct old-school releases.

For someone collecting Lightning Bolt historically rather than simply looking for a playable copy, Alpha is therefore the starting point.

LIGHTNING BOLT REPRINTS

What changes between Lightning Bolt printings?

◇

Lightning Bolt has accumulated many different printings over Magic's history, so two copies can have completely different visual identities while performing the same job in a deck.

Depending on the release, differences can include artwork, border and frame style, set symbol, rarity, foil availability, flavour text and premium treatment .

Early editions preserve the look of Magic's first years. Later versions use modern frames and different illustrations, while promotional and Secret Lair releases can transform Lightning Bolt into something much more visually distinctive.

SECRET LAIR

Lightning Bolt Secret Lair and full-art versions

◇

One of Lightning Bolt's most unusual releases arrived with Secret Lair: Mountain, Go in 2020.

Instead of including several different cards, the drop contained four different foil full-art Lightning Bolt cards , each with its own alternative artwork.

That makes Mountain, Go especially notable for collectors: the entire release was effectively a celebration of different visual interpretations of a single iconic spell.

Lightning Bolt has continued appearing in later Secret Lair releases and crossover treatments, including a FINAL FANTASY version presented as Vivi's Thunder Magic and another treatment in Avatar: The Last Airbender.

ART & TREATMENTS

Lightning Bolt artwork, foils and promo versions

◇

Lightning Bolt is particularly suited to collecting by artwork because the concept is so immediately recognisable while still leaving artists enormous freedom in how they portray it.

Some collectors prefer the historical significance of the Christopher Rush original. Others favour later illustrations, full-art treatments, foils or promotional versions.

Secret Lair has expanded that choice even further by allowing Lightning Bolt to be reinterpreted through standalone art drops and crossover releases.

Because so many versions exist, the best Lightning Bolt art is inevitably subjective — which is exactly why seeing the actual printings side by side is useful.

WHICH ONE?

Which Lightning Bolt printing should you choose?

◇

For the original: choose Limited Edition Alpha from 1993.

For old-school Magic: compare Alpha, Beta, Unlimited, Revised and other early editions to find the frame and presentation you prefer.

For a deck copy: compare modern reprints and available prices rather than paying a premium purely for an older release.

For standout artwork: compare alternative-art and Secret Lair releases, particularly versions created specifically to showcase new interpretations of Lightning Bolt.

For a premium copy: look through full-art, promotional and foil treatments before deciding which version earns the slot.
