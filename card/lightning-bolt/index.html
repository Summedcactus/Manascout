When was Lightning Bolt first printed?

◇

Lightning Bolt first appeared in Limited Edition Alpha (LEA), 5 August 1993. The original record is LEA #161, at common, illustrated by Christopher Rush. Original finish availability: nonfoil only.

The original edition predates printed collector numbers; the number above identifies the catalogue record.

HOW IT WORKS

One red mana. Three damage. More than three decades of Magic.

◇

Lightning Bolt is a one-mana red Instant with a red colour identity that deals three damage to any target. Few Magic cards communicate their purpose as cleanly.

For a single red mana, Bolt can remove a creature, finish a player or planeswalker, or damage a battle. Its efficiency helped make one mana for three damage a recognisable benchmark. The current Oracle wording governs older copies whose printed text predates modern target terminology.

The gameplay stays the same while rarity, art, frame and finish vary. A later rare showcase is not stronger than the Alpha common, and the rarity of a premium printing does not replace format legality checks.

PRINTING HISTORY

From the Alpha common to modern artwork-led releases

◇

Limited Edition Alpha (LEA) #161, released on 5 August 1993, is the first printing: a common illustrated by Christopher Rush, nonfoil-only. #161 is a modern catalogue identifier; the Alpha card does not physically print a collector number.

Beta, Unlimited, Revised and Fourth Edition continued the early Rush-art history with different edition characteristics. For an older-card collection, compare borders, corners, copyright details and language rather than treating every Rush-art copy as Alpha.

Magic 2010 #146 in 2009 offers Christopher Moeller’s illustration in a modern frame, with nonfoil and traditional foil. This is a useful representative of the modern deck-copy era, not a claim that every later printing has that painting.

Secret Lair: Mountain, Go in 2020 is unusually focused: four different foil full-art Lightning Bolts, #83–86, each with its own artist. Strixhaven Mystical Archive in 2021 adds a global showcase and Japanese alternate-art presentation with their own numbers and finish options.

The catalogue has continued into crossover imagery, including Secret Lair #1871 displayed as Vivi’s Thunder Magic and Avatar: The Last Airbender Eternal #32. These still resolve to Lightning Bolt’s underlying card identity. The explorer can expose those later records without turning this historical shortlist into an exhaustive catalogue.

ARTWORK & FRAMES

Rush, Moeller and four different Mountain, Go paintings

◇

Christopher Rush: Alpha’s image is the reference for the original Bolt. Several early editions reuse it, so artwork alone does not identify the edition.

Christopher Moeller: M10 #146 is a clear modern-frame comparison with a different illustration. Decide whether the historical Rush image or Moeller’s depiction suits the rest of your deck before comparing finish premiums.

Mountain, Go: #83 credits Noah Bradley, #84 Brigitte Roka, #85 Robbie Trevino and #86 Alexis Ziritt. These are four separate paintings of one spell, not four colours of foil applied to the same image. A set assembled by collector number documents the complete artistic idea of that drop.

Mystical Archive: global #42 credits Anato Finnstark; Japanese alternate-art #105 credits Ezoi. They are separate illustrations and frames within the same Strixhaven-associated release.

FOILS & PREMIUM FINISHES

Original nonfoil, full-art foil and etched surfaces

◇

Alpha #161 is nonfoil-only. M10 #146 offers nonfoil and traditional foil. The four Mountain, Go cards are traditional-foil-only; a nonfoil listing with one of those precise numbers needs explanation rather than an assumption that every Secret Lair has both finishes.

Mystical Archive #42 and Japanese alternate-art #105 have nonfoil, traditional foil and etched foil availability. The etched treatment is a separate surface choice, not evidence of a third painting for the same collector number.

A rare or full-art card is not automatically foil, and a digital shimmer does not reproduce the physical etched treatment. Keep the set, number, language and finish together when comparing offers from the much larger Bolt catalogue.

WHICH PRINTING?

Which Lightning Bolt printing should you choose?

◇

For the first printing: LEA #161 is the historical anchor. For other early editions, compare physical edition details rather than paying for an Alpha claim based only on Rush artwork.

For a modern deck copy: M10 #146 is a useful starting comparison, alongside later regular reprints in the explorer. Compare actual condition and offers; this guide does not rank a fixed cheapest edition.

For an art set: Mountain, Go #83–86 makes a coherent four-card display. Choose a favourite individual illustration if you do not want all four.

For a distinctive frame and finish: compare STA #42 and #105 in the same finish first, then consider whether traditional or etched foil suits that chosen painting.

◇ MANASCOUT PERSPECTIVE

ManaScout pick: a favourite Mountain, Go illustration

◇

For an artwork-led collection, start with SLD #84 by Brigitte Roka and compare it with the other three Mountain, Go images. The specific pick is subjective; the useful feature is a release that explores four different artistic approaches to the same compact spell.

For a nonfoil deck, compare M10 #146 and STA #42 instead. There is no need to select a foil-only card simply because it is our art-led starting point.
