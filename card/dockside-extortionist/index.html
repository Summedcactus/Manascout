When was Dockside Extortionist first printed?

◇

Dockside Extortionist was first printed in Commander 2019 in 2019. The original card is collector number 24 and features artwork by Forrest Imel.

It debuted in the Mystic Intellect Commander deck and quickly became one of the most recognisable red multiplayer cards ever printed.

COMMANDER LEGALITY

Is Dockside Extortionist banned in Commander?

◇

Yes. Dockside Extortionist is currently banned in Commander.

Dockside Extortionist was banned on 23 September 2024 alongside Mana Crypt, Jeweled Lotus and Nadu, Winged Wisdom.

The Commander Rules Committee highlighted Dockside's ability to become mana-positive extremely early and to generate increasingly large amounts of Treasure as multiplayer boards developed.

The card remains historically important to Commander even though it can no longer be included in decks using the official Commander banned list.

MANASCOUT GUIDE

Two mana that could create an absurd amount of mana

◇

Dockside Extortionist is a two-mana red Goblin Pirate with a 1/2 body.

When it enters the battlefield, you create a number of Treasure tokens equal to the number of artifacts and enchantments your opponents control.

In a multiplayer game, that count combines eligible permanents controlled by all of your opponents.

Because Treasure can be sacrificed for mana immediately, Dockside could easily produce more mana than the two mana required to cast it.

HOW IT WORKS

How does Dockside Extortionist count Treasures?

◇

When Dockside Extortionist enters, count the artifacts and enchantments controlled by your opponents.

You then create that many Treasure tokens.

A permanent that is both an artifact and an enchantment is counted only once, rather than once for each card type.

Your own artifacts and enchantments are not included in the calculation.

WHY IT WAS SO STRONG

Why was Dockside Extortionist so powerful?

◇

Dockside's strength came from the combination of low mana cost, multiplayer scaling and immediate Treasure production.

Commander tables naturally contain mana rocks, Equipment, enchantments, Treasure and other artifact tokens.

As more of those permanents entered play, a two-mana Dockside Extortionist could create five, ten or even more Treasure tokens from a single enters-the-battlefield trigger.

That made the card valuable both as ordinary acceleration and as an engine piece in strategies capable of repeatedly blinking, copying or returning Dockside to the battlefield.

FIRST PRINTING

The original Commander 2019 Dockside Extortionist

◇

Dockside Extortionist first appeared in Commander 2019.

The original card is collector number 24, was printed at rare and features artwork by Forrest Imel.

It was included in Mystic Intellect, the Jeskai Commander 2019 deck led by Sevinne, the Chronoclasm.

The Commander 2019 card was available only as a non-foil printing.

DOUBLE MASTERS 2022

Dockside Extortionist's first major reprint

◇

Dockside Extortionist received its first major reprint in Double Masters 2022.

The standard version is collector number 107 and features new artwork by Lie Setiawan.

Its rarity also changed from rare to mythic rare.

Unlike the Commander 2019 original, the regular Double Masters 2022 printing exists in both non-foil and traditional foil.

BORDERLESS

Double Masters 2022 borderless Dockside Extortionist

◇

Double Masters 2022 also introduced a borderless Dockside Extortionist as collector number 360.

This treatment features artwork by Douglas Shuler, giving Dockside a completely separate visual identity from both the Commander 2019 and standard Double Masters cards.

The borderless printing exists in both non-foil and traditional foil.

For collectors searching specifically for a Dockside Extortionist borderless, Double Masters 2022 #360 is the major version to compare.

FOIL-ETCHED

Dockside Extortionist foil-etched printing

◇

Double Masters 2022 collector number 452 is the foil-etched Dockside Extortionist.

It uses artwork by Lie Setiawan, matching the artist behind the regular Double Masters 2022 version.

Unlike #107 and #360, collector number #452 was available only with the etched-foil finish.

That makes it the most treatment-specific version in the Double Masters 2022 Dockside family.

THREE TREATMENTS

Which Double Masters 2022 Dockside is which?

◇

#107: regular-frame Dockside Extortionist by Lie Setiawan, available non-foil and foil.

#360: borderless Dockside Extortionist by Douglas Shuler, available non-foil and foil.

#452: foil-etched Dockside Extortionist by Lie Setiawan, available only as etched foil.

These three versions make Double Masters 2022 the most important single release for anyone comparing premium Dockside Extortionist printings.

COMMANDER BAN

Why was Dockside Extortionist banned?

◇

Dockside Extortionist was banned in Commander on 23 September 2024.

The announcement focused on explosive mana acceleration and games where early resource advantages could snowball rapidly into dominant positions.

Dockside was identified as a card that could become mana-positive as early as turn two and then scale into much larger Treasure production as a multiplayer table developed.

The ban ended Dockside's run as one of the format's defining red staples, but it also gave the card a unique historical position among modern Commander cards.

CURRENT STATUS

Is Dockside Extortionist still banned?

◇

Yes. Dockside Extortionist remains banned in Commander.

Wizards reviewed the September 2024 Commander bans in April 2025 and did not unban Dockside Extortionist, Mana Crypt or Jeweled Lotus.

Dockside continues to appear on the official Commander banned-card list.

The card can still exist as a collectible and remains legal in some other Magic formats, but it cannot be included in a deck following the official Commander rules.

AVATAR

Dockside Extortionist Avatar: The Last Airbender version

◇

Dockside Extortionist later received a Magic: The Gathering | Avatar: The Last Airbender treatment.

The Avatar: The Last Airbender Eternal version is collector number 28.

It uses a borderless Source Material presentation based on imagery from the Avatar property and exists in both non-foil and foil.

The printing arrived after Dockside's Commander ban, meaning it serves primarily as another visual and collectible version rather than restoring the card to Commander legality.

DOCKSIDE REPRINTS

What changes between Dockside Extortionist printings?

◇

Dockside Extortionist has relatively few printings compared with older Commander staples, but its versions are meaningfully different.

Differences can include artwork, rarity, traditional foil, foil-etched treatment, borderless presentation and crossover styling .

The Commander 2019 original provides the historical first printing, while Double Masters 2022 created three substantially different collector choices.

The later Avatar version then pushed the card into a completely different visual setting.

ART & TREATMENTS

Dockside Extortionist artwork, foils and borderless versions

◇

Dockside's compact printing history makes its artwork changes particularly easy to compare.

Forrest Imel defines the Commander 2019 original.

Lie Setiawan provides the standard Double Masters 2022 and foil-etched artwork, while Douglas Shuler illustrates the separate borderless treatment.

The Avatar Source Material card then provides another completely different version rooted in crossover imagery.

WHICH ONE?

Which Dockside Extortionist printing should you choose?

◇

For the original: choose Commander 2019 #24.

For the standard modern reprint: compare Double Masters 2022 #107 in non-foil or foil.

For borderless artwork: look at Douglas Shuler's Double Masters 2022 #360.

For foil-etched: choose Double Masters 2022 #452.

For crossover art: compare the Avatar: The Last Airbender Eternal #28 Source Material treatment.

For Commander play: remember that none of these printings change the card's current banned status in Commander.
