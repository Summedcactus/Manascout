Deflecting Swat

HOW IT WORKS

New targets must still be legal targets

◇

Deflecting Swat normally costs {2}{R}. If you control a commander, you may cast it without paying its mana cost. It is enough to control any commander, including one owned by another player; with two commanders, controlling one is sufficient. A commander sitting only in the command zone does not satisfy the condition.

Swat targets a spell or ability and lets you choose new targets for it. It does not counter that object, change its controller or let you select illegal targets. You also cannot change the number of targets or alter an already-established division of damage or counters among those targets.

These limits make it different from Force of Will or Counterspell. An untargeted effect does not become targeted just because Swat is involved. Wizards explains the legal-target and distribution restrictions in the Commander 2020 release notes.

The commander condition is checked while casting Swat. Players cannot interrupt that casting process by removing the commander to make you pay its normal mana cost. Casting without paying the mana cost also does not erase applicable additional costs or cost increases.

FIRST PRINTING & REPRINTS

From a nonfoil Commander card to multiple premium treatments

◇

C20 #50 identifies the original Izzy printing and its Commander 2020 release. The Arcane Maelstrom product is part of that history, rather than a reason to assume that every later copy with the same illustration is another Commander 2020 original.

Commander Masters in August 2023 expands the comparison. Standard #214 retains Izzy's art and offers nonfoil and traditional foil. Etched #532 also uses Izzy's illustration, while borderless #698 introduces Greg Staples' art and has its own nonfoil and traditional foil options.

The Artist Series: Jesper Ejsing Secret Lair arrived in February 2025. Its #1552 printing credits Ejsing and was offered in nonfoil and rainbow foil. Wizards' Winter Superdrop reveal identifies the card, artist and two editions.

Avatar: The Last Airbender Eternal followed in November 2025 with the Commander's Bundle promo, TLE #311. Patrick Ganas is the credited artist on this showcase entry, which is nonfoil only in the catalogue. Keep TLE distinct from the main Avatar set's TLA code when identifying the card.

About this guide

Deflecting Swat is redirection rather than a counterspell: it can turn a targeted spell or ability toward another legal recipient, and controlling a commander can remove its normal mana payment. Its collector history is compact enough to compare directly, from Izzy's Commander 2020 original to Greg Staples' borderless art, Jesper Ejsing's Artist Series and the Avatar crossover. Each offers a specific illustration or finish choice.

ART & TREATMENTS

Original illustration, borderless reinterpretation or crossover?

◇

If you want the original Izzy image, the main comparison is among C20 #50, CMM #214 and etched #532. Those entries let you vary release identity or finish without moving to another artist. It is a better starting point than searching for an unspecified premium Swat.

Greg Staples' CMM #698 changes the artwork and removes the conventional border. The illustration is therefore part of the choice, rather than treating borderless as a finish that can be selected for every other version. Both nonfoil and traditional foil exist for this entry.

Ejsing's #1552 changes the artist again and belongs to a drop curated around his work. Compare it with #698 if you want an alternative illustration while retaining a choice of nonfoil or foil presentation. The drop's rainbow-foil label identifies its edition; it is not a name for the CMM etched surface.

The Ganas Avatar card supplies a setting-specific interpretation, and its flavour text invokes Iroh's lightning redirection. That thematic connection can make it appealing to an Avatar collector independently of whether another printing has a more elaborate finish. The complete card image and current text are available in the shared explorer.

FOILS & PREMIUM FINISHES

The original and Avatar entries are nonfoil; later releases supply foils

◇

C20 #50 is nonfoil only. A foil with Izzy's artwork belongs to a later entry: traditional foil CMM #214 or etched CMM #532. A listing that describes an original Commander 2020 foil needs careful identification rather than an assumption that every release offered every finish.

For Staples' artwork, CMM #698 has nonfoil and traditional foil. Ejsing's SLD #1552 has nonfoil and rainbow-foil editions. The art, number and finish should be recorded together, because those descriptions are not interchangeable with etched #532.

TLE #311 is nonfoil only in its catalogue record. The existence of foils elsewhere in the Avatar product range does not establish a foil version of this particular card. Use the exact entry when comparing crossover options.

ManaScout's foil animation illustrates a preview rather than reproducing the physical etched or rainbow surface. For a finish-focused purchase, compare photographs and condition alongside the source record and current price fields.

WHICH ONE?

Which Deflecting Swat printing should you choose?

◇

For the debut: choose C20 #50 in nonfoil. It represents the original Commander product and Izzy presentation, regardless of what other versions currently cost.

For a deck within a set budget: compare the currently available nonfoils, including C20 #50, CMM #214 and the alternative-art entries. Hold condition and language consistent, and do not assume the borderless copy must always carry a premium in every listing.

For the original art in foil: compare CMM #214 traditional foil with etched #532. For a new illustration, compare Staples' #698 and Ejsing's #1552 before selecting the finish.

For Avatar collecting: TLE #311 supplies the Ganas artwork and Iroh connection. It is a nonfoil choice, so decide whether the crossover identity matters more to you than having a foil effect.

REVIEWED COLLECTOR NOTES

A practical comparison path

◇

Begin the artwork comparison with C20 #50, CMM #698 and SLD #1552 in nonfoil. That keeps the question about Izzy, Staples or Ejsing rather than comparing a basic scan against an animated foil. If Izzy wins, return to CMM #214 and etched #532 for the finish decision. The original product identity is still C20, even when a later card has the same picture.

For a finish-led collection, distinguish the source labels. Commander Masters traditional foil and etched foil are different treatments. Ejsing’s Artist Series was sold in nonfoil and rainbow-foil editions; the catalogue represents the reflective edition as foil. That product description does not turn the CMM etched card into a rainbow foil.

The Avatar Eternal version is another artist-led choice, not a missing foil option to be assumed from nearby products. TLE #311 credits Ganas and is nonfoil-only in the checked record. Keep the TLE code alongside the number; TLA identifies the main Avatar set and is not interchangeable.

For an Edgar Markov deck, Swat’s alternative cost still requires a commander on the battlefield when casting it. Eminence working from the command zone does not satisfy that condition. Likewise, a new printing cannot turn an untargeted board wipe into a targeted spell that Swat can redirect. These distinctions matter more to a protection package than the finish.
