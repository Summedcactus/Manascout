PRINTING IDENTIFICATION

Which Sol Ring printing is this?

◇

Original Sol Ring: LEA #269 is the Alpha catalogue identity. Early cards do not print that collector number on the face; familiar Tedin artwork and a black border alone are not authentication.

Familiar Commander art: CMD #261 and CMM #410 credit Mike Bierek. The former is nonfoil; the latter has nonfoil and traditional foil. V10 #12 is a separate foil-only boxed edition.

“Mark Poole Sol Ring” or “full art”: check CMM #703, the borderless Poole illustration. It is neither the original Tedin image nor Bierek’s standard-frame #410, and it offers both ordinary finishes.

Ornate or Tolkien-themed: MPS #24 is Baga’s foil-only Invention. LTC #408 and #410 are Quenya-language Sol Rings; a serialized #408z has its own physical serial and double-rainbow treatment. None is The One Ring.

HOW IT WORKS

Two colourless mana, with no colour fixing

◇

Sol Ring costs {1} and taps to produce {C}{C}. It can therefore help cast a spell with a substantial generic cost, but it does not produce white, blue, black, red or green mana. In particular, it cannot pay a coloured symbol simply because it produces two mana.

Unlike Mana Vault, Sol Ring has no ability stopping its normal untap. Unlike Arcane Signet, it is not a way to find a colour in your commander's identity. Those differences explain why the three artifacts serve different purposes despite often being grouped together as mana rocks.

Early copies use wording and mana notation from their period. The current rules text in the shared explorer provides the common reference for comparing them; a different frame, rarity symbol or language does not change what Sol Ring does. Check current format legality before selecting a copy for a particular deck.

FIRST PRINTING & REPRINTS

From Alpha to the Commander-era illustration

◇

The Alpha printing connects Sol Ring directly to Magic's debut. Beta, Unlimited and Revised followed with the original Mark Tedin illustration. Those are separate editions, so an old copy with familiar artwork is not automatically an Alpha copy. Border, corners, printing details and the actual edition all matter when identifying an early card.

From the Vault: Relics #12 in 2010 uses Mike Bierek's illustration in a foil-only release. Commander 2011 #261 uses that artist in nonfoil, providing a particularly useful comparison between a premium boxed release and a preconstructed-deck printing. Neither should be described as the 1993 original merely because it depicts the same artifact.

Kaladesh Inventions #24 arrived in September 2016 with Volkan Baga's art and an ornate frame. Commander Masters in August 2023 supplies another clear pair: standard #410 by Bierek and borderless #703 by Mark Poole. Choosing between those two changes the illustration as well as its surrounding presentation.

About this guide

Sol Ring first appeared in Limited Edition Alpha in August 1993, at uncommon with Mark Tedin’s artwork; catalogues identify it as LEA #269. The original is nonfoil. This guide groups the important versions into five collecting families: early originals, Mike Bierek’s Commander-era image, Volkan Baga’s Invention, Mark Poole’s borderless art and the Middle-earth Rings of Power. A long run of Commander reprints explains many familiar copies; a new set symbol does not always mean a new painting.

ART & TREATMENTS

The artist matters more than the word “premium”

◇

Tedin's original is the reference point for an early-Magic collection. Bierek's version links From the Vault to the Commander era, while Baga's Invention places the artifact inside Kaladesh's decorative design language. Poole's CMM #703 is another illustration, rather than simply a larger crop of Bierek's #410.

The Middle-earth Rings of Power are a separate family. Wizards identifies Elven, Dwarven and Human themes, written in Quenya with Tengwar letterforms. The Elven #408 credits Randy Gallegos; the Human #410 credits Anastasia Balakchina. These are still Sol Ring, not alternative printings of the mechanically different The One Ring.

This is a curated set of comparisons rather than an attempt to list every Commander or Secret Lair release. If you collect a particular artist or crossover, use the archive's set, artist and language fields to locate that entry before comparing its finish.

FOILS & PREMIUM FINISHES

A foil Ring is not necessarily a serialized ring

◇

Alpha has no official foil version. V10 #12 and MPS #24 are foil-only choices from different releases and artists; neither is an original Alpha foil. For a contemporary choice with both nonfoil and traditional foil availability, compare CMM #410 or #703.

The Middle-earth themed rings separate nonfoil and serialized double rainbow foil editions. Wizards specifies 3,000 nonfoil Elven, 7,000 Dwarven and 9,000 Human rings, alongside serialized foil runs of 300, 700 and 900 respectively. These published quantities apply to those particular versions, not to all Sol Rings with Tolkien artwork.

The Elven serialized entry is catalogued as LTC #408z, while the nonfoil is #408. The suffix is a database distinction; an individual serialized copy is identified by its physical serial stamp. Wizards' first-look announcement explains the three families and their quantities.

ManaScout's shimmer illustrates a foil preview. It cannot reproduce the actual Invention surface or double rainbow effect, and it cannot verify condition or a serial stamp. Compare the exact printing record with photographs of the offered card.

ManaScout’s digital foil animation is illustrative, not a photograph of the physical finish. Use real photographs to judge reflectivity, texture and condition.

WHICH ONE?

Which Sol Ring printing should you choose?

◇

For Magic's beginning: choose Alpha, after checking the edition carefully. An early reprint with Tedin's artwork can suit a historical collection too, but it represents a different release.

For a deck with the Commander-era image: compare CMD #261 and standard later Bierek reprints. Use current price fields and condition instead of assuming that a particular product always supplies the cheapest copy.

For an ornate artifact collection: MPS #24 offers a distinct artist and Masterpiece frame. For a borderless illustration in a choice of nonfoil or traditional foil, CMM #703 is a more direct comparison.

For Tolkien collecting: decide which people and artwork you prefer before choosing nonfoil or serialization. A numbered Elven ring, an unnumbered Human ring and The One Ring belong to different comparisons.

REVIEWED COLLECTOR NOTES

A practical comparison path

◇

Sol Ring’s editions are easiest to understand as three different collecting projects. A first-edition collection asks whether the physical card is Alpha, Beta, Unlimited or another early printing. An artwork collection asks whether you want Tedin, Bierek, Baga, Poole or a setting-specific reinterpretation. A finish collection asks about nonfoil, product-specific foil or a serialized premium object. Keeping those questions separate avoids treating every reflective Ring as the same upgrade.

For a controlled comparison, place CMD #261 nonfoil beside CMM #410 nonfoil before introducing the borderless #703. The first pair tests release identity with Bierek’s image; the next comparison introduces Poole’s illustration. Add the Invention only if Kaladesh’s frame and Baga’s painting are actually the attraction. This is more informative than ranking every version under one “premium” label.

The Elven #408 and Human #410 in LTC are mechanically Sol Ring, whereas The One Ring is a different card. A Quenya-language ring must not be substituted for that legendary draw engine. Verify code, number, language and serialization separately; the z suffix is a catalogue distinction, not an invitation to invent extra finishes.

As reviewed on 10 November 2026, Wizards lists Sol Ring as banned in Legacy and restricted in Vintage. Current catalogue data lists it as legal in Commander. In a Commander mana base, compare its colourless output with Command Tower and Arcane Signet rather than assuming they solve the same colour requirements.

COLLECTOR COMPARISON

Choose artwork, edition and finish separately

◇

For a historical binder, separate edition identification from artwork preference. Alpha, later early reprints and an old-looking premium frame are different collecting projects. This guide deliberately does not turn a catalogue index or one photographed corner into a guarantee of authenticity.

For a deck, start with the illustration and choose finish second. Compare Bierek’s CMD #261 and CMM #410 in nonfoil before adding Poole’s #703. Then compare Arcane Signet’s Brawl original and later artworks: its newer crystal history is a useful contrast to Sol Ring’s decades of reprints. Dark Ritual offers another Alpha-era art collection, but its burst-mana rules and original artist are different.

For premium collecting, identify what the word “foil” is doing. An Invention, a boxed-product foil and a serialized Quenya ring are different objects. Their availability does not make a conventional Commander reprint incomplete. Use the full set/number/finish identity rather than a universal best-version or value ranking.
