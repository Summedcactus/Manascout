RULES & FORMAT CONTEXT

Living Death in a deck

◇

Living Death costs {3}{B}{B}. First each player exiles all creature cards from their graveyard; then each sacrifices all creatures they control; finally the cards exiled by the first instruction return to the battlefield. It is a symmetrical effect, not a one-sided reanimation spell.

Creatures sacrificed during the second instruction are not among the cards exiled by the first, so they do not immediately return through this spell. Indestructible does not prevent sacrifice. Players do not receive priority between these instructions while the spell resolves.

That sequence rewards graveyard preparation and makes opponents’ graveyards relevant. Worldly Tutor finds a creature for the library’s top rather than the graveyard; Skullclamp turns appropriate creature deaths into draw. They support different resource exchanges, not interchangeable forms of reanimation.

PRINTING HISTORY

From the debut to later physical editions

◇

Tempest #142, released on 14 October 1997, is the debut: rare, Charles Gillespie, nonfoil only. A foil with a later edition code should not be advertised as an original Tempest booster foil.

Masters 25 #96 arrived in March 2018 with Mark Winters’ illustration and both nonfoil and traditional foil. It is a useful modern reference for that art, rather than a claim to be the first ever foil or the first appearance of the painting.

Warhammer 40,000 Commander introduced L J Koh’s illustration in October 2022. The ordinary deck record is #202 and nonfoil-only; the Collector’s Edition record is #202★ and surge-foil-only. The star matters when identifying the physical version.

Tarkir: Dragonstorm Commander #185 followed in April 2025, returning to Winters’ art in a nonfoil-only record. These are selected physical comparisons, not a complete chronology of every boxed or Commander reprint. Vintage Masters belongs to Magic Online and is not an additional paper edition.

About this guide

Living Death’s original is a nonfoil Tempest rare by Charles Gillespie. Later printings provide Mark Winters’ artwork in ordinary finishes and L J Koh’s Warhammer interpretation in separate nonfoil and surge-foil records. The card’s dramatic reputation comes from a precise, symmetrical sequence of graveyard exile, sacrifice and return; choosing a premium edition does not change which creatures actually come back.

ARTWORK & FRAME COMPARISON

Which differences change the illustration?

◇

Gillespie’s Tempest illustration is the historical anchor. Winters and Koh provide two later credited interpretations, so those are genuine artwork changes rather than just new expansion symbols around the original.

A25 #96 and TDC #185 let you compare later products with Winters’ artwork. Keep their finish menus separate: Masters 25 supports traditional foil, while the listed Tarkir deck record does not.

The Warhammer pair holds Koh’s artwork broadly constant while changing physical finish and catalogue identity. This is a useful controlled comparison if you like the illustration but are deciding between ordinary deck and Collector’s Edition surfaces.

FOILS & SPECIAL TREATMENTS

Choose the printing before its finish

◇

The original TMP #142 is nonfoil-only. For a traditional foil of the Winters artwork, A25 #96 is one verified option. It is not an etched or textured edition simply because it is a Masters printing.

For surge foil, identify 40K #202★ explicitly. Do not apply that finish to #202 or assume that every Warhammer product card can be switched between nonfoil and surge without changing its exact record. Inspect physical photographs when comparing the moving reflective pattern.

MANASCOUT RECOMMENDATIONS

Which printing suits your collection?

◇

Original-edition collection: TMP #142 nonfoil preserves the Tempest debut and Gillespie credit. Confirm the set rather than relying on an old-looking frame.

Modern art with a finish choice: A25 #96 offers Winters’ illustration in nonfoil or traditional foil. TDC #185 is a later nonfoil comparison with that artist.

Setting-led deck: Koh’s Warhammer image offers a distinct visual identity. Choose #202 for nonfoil or #202★ for the verified surge surface.

Deck construction: choose the actual card for its symmetrical rules before curating its edition in Deck Studio. Individual and bulk printing choices preserve gameplay identity; they do not repair an unsuitable graveyard plan.
