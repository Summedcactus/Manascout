◇

Rhystic Study is a blue enchantment first released in Prophecy in 2000. Its tax-or-draw ability has since made it one of the most recognisable card-advantage engines in multiplayer Magic.

Whenever an opponent casts a spell, Rhystic Study gives that player the option to pay an additional mana. If they do not, its controller may draw a card — producing the question Commander players have heard countless times: “Do you pay the one?”

Its rise from an older-set enchantment into a major Commander staple has also produced a growing range of printings, artwork and collector treatments to compare.

PRINTING HISTORY

When was Rhystic Study first printed?

◇

Rhystic Study was first printed in Prophecy in 2000. The Prophecy release is therefore the original Rhystic Study printing and predates its later reputation as one of blue's best-known Commander staples.

Since that original release, Rhystic Study has returned in multiple Magic products and received alternative artwork, different frames and premium finishes.

That history gives players a meaningful choice between the original presentation, later reprints and modern collector-focused versions.

REPRINTS & TREATMENTS

What changes between Rhystic Study printings?

◇

Rhystic Study has moved far beyond its original Prophecy presentation. Later versions can differ through artwork, frame design, set identity, foil finish and other collector-focused treatments.

Some players prefer the character of an older printing, while others want alternative artwork or a more elaborate version for a favourite Commander deck.

WHICH ONE?

Which Rhystic Study printing should you choose?

◇

For a deck copy: compare the lower-priced available reprints first if your main priority is simply playing Rhystic Study.

For the original: the Prophecy printing is the place to start if you want the version tied directly to the card's first release.

For artwork: Rhystic Study is particularly worth comparing visually, as later versions can offer dramatically different interpretations of the card.

For collecting: premium finishes and special treatments can provide something much more distinctive than a standard reprint.
