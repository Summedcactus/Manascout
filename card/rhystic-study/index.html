PRINTING IDENTIFICATION

Which Rhystic Study printing is this?

◇

Original or original-art reprint? PCY #45 is Prophecy, with both finishes. JMP #169 reuses Nielsen’s image in nonfoil; CM1 #15 is a foil-only Commander’s Arsenal edition. The same artist does not mean the same release.

Different painting? J18 #7 credits Canavan and is foil-only. J22 #114 credits Kirgetova and is nonfoil-only. Neither is simply a new frame around the Prophecy illustration.

Enchanting Tales? WOT #25 is Malyon’s showcase art. #71 is Fuzichoco’s anime image in nonfoil or traditional foil; #91 is the separate confetti record using that anime art. A glittering scan is not enough to distinguish them.

Final Fantasy? FCA #31 credits Square Enix and supports nonfoil/traditional foil. Its mythic rarity belongs to that later record, not the original Prophecy common.

HOW IT WORKS

From Prophecy common to Commander icon

◇

Rhystic Study costs {2}{U}, is an Enchantment and has a blue colour identity. Its tax-or-draw ability has made it one of the most recognisable card-advantage engines in multiplayer Magic.

Whenever an opponent casts a spell, Rhystic Study triggers. As that ability resolves, the opponent can pay {1}; if they do not, you may draw a card. The payment is not an extra cost required to cast their spell. The trigger resolves before the spell that caused it and still exists if that spell is countered.

That simple choice produced one of Commander’s familiar table questions: “Do you pay the one?” It is the same gameplay question on a Prophecy common and an Enchanting Tales mythic. Rarity belongs to a particular printing, not to an unchanging rule for the card.

PRINTING HISTORY

The first printing is common; later rarity changes are real

◇

Prophecy (PCY) #45 was released on 5 June 2000 at common, illustrated by Terese Nielsen. Both nonfoil and traditional foil were available. “Original rare” is incorrect for this record even though some later editions are rare or mythic rare.

Commander’s Arsenal #15 followed in 2012 with Nielsen’s illustration in a modern frame and foil-only finish; its catalogue rarity remains common. The 2018 Judge #7 changes the illustration to Paul Scott Canavan’s and is a rare, foil-only promotional edition.

Jumpstart #169 in 2020 keeps Nielsen’s artwork but is rare and nonfoil-only. Jumpstart 2022 #114 is also a nonfoil rare, with Tatiana Kirgetova artwork. The List’s PCY-45 record instead reproduces the earlier Prophecy face; do not confuse the reprint mark with a first-edition copy.

Wilds of Eldraine: Enchanting Tales arrived on 8 September 2023. #25, #71 and #91 are mythic rare records from the same release, with #91 reserved for confetti foil. Their later rarity does not change the original Prophecy rarity. The wider catalogue also includes Riot Games’ Secret Lair #478 and the Final Fantasy: Through the Ages record #31; the shortlist deliberately concentrates on the clearest historical and finish comparisons.

About this guide

Rhystic Study first appeared in Prophecy on 5 June 2000: PCY #45, a common illustrated by Terese Nielsen, available in nonfoil and traditional foil. The important later art families are Nielsen reprints, Paul Scott Canavan’s Judge art, Tatiana Kirgetova’s Jumpstart image, Serena Malyon’s showcase, Fuzichoco’s anime art and the Final Fantasy crossover. This guide shows which printing is which before asking you to navigate the full archive.

ARTWORK & FRAMES

Nielsen, Canavan, Kirgetova, Malyon and Fuzichoco

◇

Original artwork: PCY #45, CM1 #15 and JMP #169 preserve Nielsen’s image. Frame and finish can change while the painting remains recognisable. The older frame of #45 is a separate preference from the surface of a particular copy.

Alternate standalone illustrations: Canavan’s Judge #7 and Kirgetova’s J22 #114 offer different artistic interpretations. The Judge card requires a foil preference; the Jumpstart 2022 card gives a nonfoil alternative.

Enchanting Tales: Serena Malyon illustrates showcase #25. Fuzichoco illustrates the borderless anime #71 and its confetti counterpart #91. The anime painting is shared between those last two numbers; confetti is a treatment change, not a third Enchanting Tales painting.

Final Fantasy: Through the Ages: FCA #31, released on 13 June 2025, credits Square Enix and offers a borderless crossover illustration. It has nonfoil and traditional foil versions; it is a later reprint, separate from the three WOT treatment records.

FOILS & PREMIUM FINISHES

An original foil, Judge foil or confetti surface?

◇

PCY #45 is available in nonfoil and traditional foil, so an original-frame foil is a real option. CM1 #15 and J18 #7 are foil-only. JMP #169 and J22 #114 are nonfoil-only; missing foil prices should not be used to manufacture a foil edition.

Within WOT, #25 and #71 offer nonfoil and traditional foil. #91 is confetti foil only, using Fuzichoco’s anime art. A foil #71 is therefore not the same collector record as #91. Check the number and surface rather than accepting a listing described only as “anime foil.”

Rarity and finish are separate fields. The original common has a real foil, while later rare Jumpstart records do not. A current market premium is not evidence that the original card was printed at rare.

ManaScout’s digital foil animation is illustrative, not a photograph of the physical finish. Use real photographs to judge reflectivity, texture and condition.

WHICH PRINTING?

Which Rhystic Study printing should you choose?

◇

For the first printing: choose PCY #45. Nonfoil retains the older-frame look without a reflective surface; traditional foil keeps the same original release identity.

For the original art in a later frame: compare JMP #169 nonfoil and CM1 #15 foil. Neither is a first Prophecy printing.

For an alternate-art deck copy: compare J22 #114 with WOT #25 or #71 nonfoil. They give meaningful artistic choices rather than simply a different set symbol.

For a specific premium: choose J18 #7 for Canavan’s Judge art or WOT #91 for anime confetti. Identify the edition before comparing condition and actual offers; this guide makes no fixed cheapest-printing claim.

◇ MANASCOUT PERSPECTIVE

ManaScout pick: compare PCY #45 with WOT #71 nonfoil

◇

Our first pair is Prophecy #45 nonfoil and Enchanting Tales #71 nonfoil. Holding finish constant exposes the real artwork and frame change across more than two decades. It is a useful way to decide whether history or the anime interpretation matters more to you.

If the anime image wins, consider #71 traditional foil before #91 confetti. Those are distinct finish choices for the same painting; the ordinary foil is not an incomplete version of the confetti card.

COLLECTOR COMPARISON

Choose artwork, edition and finish separately

◇

The practical identification order is set, collector number, artist, then finish. That resolves common descriptions such as “original art foil”, “anime Rhystic” or “Jumpstart version” into a specific record. A price premium or later mythic symbol does not change the first-printing history.

For an original-art foil comparison, keep PCY #45 and CM1 #15 side by side and inspect real photographs. For an illustration comparison, use PCY #45 and WOT #71 nonfoil first. Only then compare ordinary anime foil with #91 confetti; the artwork is shared while the physical treatment differs.
