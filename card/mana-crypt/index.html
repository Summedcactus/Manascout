Mana Crypt

Explore Mana Crypt from its unusual 1995 HarperPrism book-promo origin through Judge promos, Eternal Masters, Kaladesh Inventions, Double Masters borderless art and The Lost Caverns of Ixalan neon-ink treatments. Compare its printings, artwork and current Commander ban status.

QUICK ANSWER

What was the first Mana Crypt printing?

◇

Mana Crypt first appeared as a HarperPrism book promo in 1995. The original printing is collector number 5 and features artwork by Mark Tedin.

Unlike a normal expansion-set card, Mana Crypt was originally connected to the Magic novel Final Sacrifice. Players could obtain the promotional card through the book-promotion programme.

That makes Mana Crypt's first printing fundamentally different from the origins of cards such as Sol Ring or Mana Vault: it began life as a promotional card rather than inside a conventional Magic set.

COMMANDER LEGALITY

Is Mana Crypt banned in Commander?

◇

Yes. Mana Crypt is currently banned in Commander.

Mana Crypt was banned on 23 September 2024 alongside Dockside Extortionist, Jeweled Lotus and Nadu, Winged Wisdom.

The ban targeted Mana Crypt's ability to create extremely explosive openings by producing two colourless mana despite costing zero mana to cast.

It remains on the official Commander banned list.

MANASCOUT GUIDE

Zero mana in. Two mana out.

◇

Mana Crypt is a zero-mana artifact that taps to produce two colourless mana.

That means it can accelerate your available mana immediately, without requiring any mana investment to put it onto the battlefield.

The drawback appears at the beginning of your upkeep: you flip a coin, and if you lose the flip, Mana Crypt deals three damage to you.

For much of its history, that life-loss risk was considered a relatively small price for the extraordinary acceleration the artifact could provide.

HOW IT WORKS

How does Mana Crypt's coin flip work?

◇

At the beginning of your upkeep, Mana Crypt creates a triggered ability that instructs you to flip a coin.

If you lose the flip, Mana Crypt deals three damage to you.

Winning the flip does not give you an additional benefit; it simply means you avoid the damage that turn.

The artifact's mana ability is separate: tapping Mana Crypt produces two colourless mana whenever you can legally activate it.

FAST MANA

Why was Mana Crypt so powerful in Commander?

◇

Mana Crypt could produce two mana immediately while costing nothing to cast.

An opening hand containing Mana Crypt could therefore jump far ahead of the normal mana curve before another player had even taken their first turn.

The 2024 Commander ban announcement specifically highlighted starts where Mana Crypt enabled a player to untap with around five mana on turn two after deploying additional mana rocks.

In shorter games, the possibility of taking three damage from failed coin flips often did little to offset the advantage created by that early burst of mana.

FIRST PRINTING

The original HarperPrism Mana Crypt

◇

Mana Crypt's first paper appearance was not in a booster set.

It was released as a HarperPrism Book Promo connected with the Magic novel Final Sacrifice in 1995.

The original card is collector number 5 and features artwork by Mark Tedin.

If you are searching for the first Mana Crypt printing, this HarperPrism promotional card is the version you want, not Eternal Masters or one of the later premium editions.

PROMO ORIGINS

Why was Mana Crypt a book promo?

◇

During Magic's early years, selected novels were connected to promotional cards obtainable through book coupons and mail-in offers.

Mana Crypt was associated with Final Sacrifice, part of the early Magic fiction line published by HarperPrism.

That unusual distribution method explains why Mana Crypt existed for years without belonging to a normal tabletop expansion.

It also gives the original printing a very different collecting history from conventional early Magic cards.

JUDGE PROMO

The 2011 Mana Crypt Judge Promo

◇

Mana Crypt received a major paper collector version as a Judge Gift Card in 2011.

The Judge Promo is collector number 5 and features artwork by Matt Stewart.

It was produced only as a foil and introduced artwork that would later become closely associated with several Mana Crypt reprints.

For collectors, it forms an important bridge between Mana Crypt's obscure book-promo origin and the major booster-product reprints that followed.

ETERNAL MASTERS

Mana Crypt in Eternal Masters

◇

Eternal Masters in 2016 was one of the most important Mana Crypt reprints.

Mana Crypt appeared as collector number 225, at mythic rare, with artwork by Matt Stewart.

Unlike the original HarperPrism promo and the foil-only Judge version, Eternal Masters made Mana Crypt available in both non-foil and foil.

The Eternal Masters printing later also became the basis for versions appearing through products such as The List and Mystery Booster.

KALADESH INVENTIONS

The Mana Crypt Masterpiece printing

◇

Mana Crypt was selected for Kaladesh Inventions in 2016.

The Masterpiece printing is collector number 16 and features artwork by Volkan Baǵa.

Kaladesh Inventions used an ornate artifact-focused frame and premium foil treatment that makes this Mana Crypt immediately distinguishable from conventional versions.

For collectors specifically searching for a Mana Crypt Invention or Mana Crypt Masterpiece, this is the key printing.

DOUBLE MASTERS

Mana Crypt returns in Double Masters

◇

Double Masters brought Mana Crypt back again in 2020.

The standard printing is collector number 270, is a mythic rare and uses Matt Stewart artwork.

Both non-foil and traditional-foil versions exist.

Double Masters also gave Mana Crypt a completely separate borderless treatment, turning the release into another important point in the card's collector history.

BORDERLESS

Double Masters borderless Mana Crypt

◇

Double Masters collector number 361 is the set's borderless Mana Crypt.

It features completely different artwork by Ron Spencer.

Both non-foil and foil versions were produced, with foil copies associated with the premium Double Masters VIP product.

The Ron Spencer version is one of the clearest examples of Mana Crypt moving away from ordinary reprint presentation and becoming a collector-focused showcase card.

LOST CAVERNS OF IXALAN

Mana Crypt Special Guests printing

◇

Mana Crypt became one of the headline cards for the debut of Special Guests alongside The Lost Caverns of Ixalan in 2023.

The base Special Guests printing is collector number 17 and features artwork by Dominik Mayer.

It uses a dramatic borderless treatment and exists in both non-foil and traditional foil.

That alone would have made it a major new Mana Crypt version, but Wizards pushed the treatment substantially further through Cosmium neon ink.

COSMIUM NEON INK

Mana Crypt neon-ink versions

◇

The Lost Caverns of Ixalan Collector Boosters introduced multiple Cosmium neon-ink Mana Crypts.

These versions use Dominik Mayer's borderless concept while changing the neon-ink colour and elements of the artwork to match each treatment.

They appear across collector numbers 17a through 17f alongside the standard Special Guests #17 version.

The rarest treatments became some of the most recognisable modern Mana Crypt collector cards and gave the card an unusually large family of visually related premium variants.

COMMANDER BAN

Why was Mana Crypt banned in Commander?

◇

Mana Crypt was banned in Commander on 23 September 2024.

The announcement focused on the format's increasingly explosive early-game starts.

Because Mana Crypt costs zero and immediately produces two mana, it can effectively allow its controller to skip several stages of ordinary mana development.

The Commander Rules Committee argued that while the three-damage coin-flip drawback could matter in very long games, it was much less meaningful when early acceleration contributed to games ending around turns six to eight.

CURRENT STATUS

Is Mana Crypt still banned?

◇

Yes. Mana Crypt remains banned in Commander.

The official Magic banned and restricted list continues to include Mana Crypt among the cards banned from Commander.

Mana Crypt is also restricted in Vintage, meaning Vintage decks may include only one copy.

The legality of the card does not alter its printing history, and its unusual origin and premium treatments continue to make Mana Crypt a significant collector card.

MANA CRYPT REPRINTS

What changes between Mana Crypt printings?

◇

Mana Crypt has one of the more unusual printing histories among famous Magic artifacts.

Its versions differ through artwork, promotional origin, frame design, foil treatment, Masterpiece presentation, borderless styling and neon ink .

The HarperPrism card represents Mana Crypt's unusual beginning, while Eternal Masters offers a conventional modern-set printing.

Kaladesh Inventions, Double Masters and The Lost Caverns of Ixalan then take the card progressively further into premium collector territory.

ART & TREATMENTS

Mana Crypt artwork, borderless cards and foils

◇

Mana Crypt's artworks now span several completely different eras of Magic.

Mark Tedin created the original HarperPrism artwork. Matt Stewart illustrated the version used by the Judge Promo, Eternal Masters and later reprints.

Volkan Baǵa created the ornate Kaladesh Invention interpretation, while Ron Spencer illustrated the Double Masters borderless version.

Dominik Mayer then provided the Special Guests artwork and related neon-ink variants for The Lost Caverns of Ixalan.

That makes Mana Crypt especially well suited to visual comparison: many of its most important versions look completely different from one another.

WHICH ONE?

Which Mana Crypt printing should you choose?

◇

For the first printing: choose the 1995 HarperPrism Book Promo #5.

For classic foil history: compare the 2011 Judge Gift Card.

For a conventional modern printing: Eternal Masters #225 is one of the key versions.

For a Masterpiece: look at Kaladesh Inventions #16.

For borderless artwork: compare Ron Spencer's Double Masters #361.

For modern premium art: look at Special Guests #17 from The Lost Caverns of Ixalan.

For the wildest collector treatment: compare the Cosmium neon-ink Special Guests variants.

For Commander: remember that every printing represents the same currently banned card.
