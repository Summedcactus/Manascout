HOW IT WORKS

Three mana once, not a reusable mana rock

◇

Black Lotus costs {0}, is an Artifact and has a colourless colour identity. Tapping and sacrificing it produces three mana of one colour. Its ability to turn a card into immediate acceleration explains its place among the Power Nine.

The same rules identity does not give every collector reproduction permission for sanctioned play. As checked on 4 November 2026, Black Lotus is restricted in Vintage and banned in Commander and Legacy. An Alpha copy is not Commander-legal, and a commemorative copy is not a tournament substitute for a Vintage copy.

PRINTING HISTORY

Alpha, Beta and Unlimited are three distinct 1993 editions

◇

Limited Edition Alpha #232 is the debut, catalogued at rare with Christopher Rush art and a release date of 5 August 1993. Beta #233 follows on 4 October 1993, then Unlimited #233 on 1 December 1993. All three are nonfoil.

Those collector numbers are catalogue identifiers: early Alpha, Beta and Unlimited cards do not print modern collector numbers on their faces. A seller showing “#233” has not by that alone established Beta versus Unlimited.

Collectors’ Edition (CED) #233 and International Collectors’ Edition (CEI) #233 are catalogued on 10 December 1993. They are official collector products with square corners and special gold-bordered backs. Their different product backs, including International identification on CEI, matter even when the card front looks familiar.

30th Anniversary Edition #228 and retro-frame #525 were released in 2022 as a commemorative product with a special back. Wizards explicitly describes the product as non-tournament-legal. These are later reproductions of the famous card, not a return of Black Lotus to ordinary booster-set tournament play.

ARTWORK & FRAMES

One classic painting, several frames and physical identifiers

◇

Christopher Rush is the illustrator for the seven paper records in this shortlist. Alpha and Beta have black borders, while Unlimited has a white border. Alpha’s more rounded corners are a useful visual clue; edition identification still requires inspecting the whole card.

Collectors’ Editions introduce square corners and a gold-bordered back. The gold here describes the collector-product back and should not be confused with a normal gold-bordered face or a premium foil finish.

30A #228 uses a modern frame and #525 uses a retro frame. Both retain Rush’s artwork in the commemorative product. Neither should be mistaken for an original 1993 copy because of a retro-looking face.

The wider catalogue includes Chris Rahn’s digital Vintage Masters #4 and oversized championship-art records. They are meaningful artwork history, but an MTGO entry or a trophy-size display is not another ordinary-sized playable paper Lotus.

FOILS & PREMIUM FINISHES

No foil among these original and commemorative paper entries

◇

Alpha, Beta, Unlimited, CED, CEI and both shortlisted 30A numbers are nonfoil-only. There is no original Alpha pack foil. A metallic-looking digital render, gold-bordered back or premium asking price does not establish foil availability.

Black Lotus is on the Reserved List. Read the policy’s tournament-legal, digital and special-purpose distinctions alongside the actual product description; a commemorative reproduction is not proof of a normal legal reprint.

WHICH PRINTING?

Which Black Lotus edition should you research first?

◇

For the first printing: Alpha is the historical anchor. Establish the edition from physical characteristics and expert examination, not a number copied from a listing.

For the early black-border era: compare Alpha and Beta. If the goal is an early white-border edition, Unlimited is the relevant comparison.

For official collector-product history: compare CED and CEI with clear photographs of their backs and corners. Their purpose and play status remain different from the original tournament-era editions.

For a modern commemorative display: compare 30A #228 and #525 for frame preference. The special back and non-tournament-legal status are central to the choice.

For any substantial purchase: edition, condition, authenticity and seller reputation matter more than the lowest displayed price. ManaScout’s catalogue and price fields do not authenticate individual cards.
