RULES & FORMAT CONTEXT

Brainstorm in a deck

◇

Brainstorm draws three cards, then puts two cards from your hand on top of your library. This guide is about the versions of that card; a different painting does not change that sequence.

As reviewed on 10 November 2026, Wizards restricts Brainstorm in Vintage. The catalogue lists it legal in Commander and Legacy. A Mystical Archive printing does not grant legality in every format associated with Strixhaven. Compare Counterspell and Force of Will for other long-running blue artwork collections.

PRINTING HISTORY

From the debut to later physical editions

◇

Ice Age #61 dates to 3 June 1995. Rush’s illustration and the older frame identify the starting point, but the original is not a booster foil. Later rarity changes do not turn that first common into a rare.

Mercadian Masques #61 followed in October 1999 with DiTerlizzi’s artwork and both nonfoil and traditional foil. Its collector number happens to match the Ice Age record. Always read ICE or MMQ with #61; the number alone cannot distinguish them. The Masques foil is a genuine old-border reflective option, without pretending to be an original Ice Age foil.

Masters 25 #46, released in March 2018, credits Chris Rahn and offers both finishes. Its Ice Age watermark points to the card’s historical origin; it does not mean that this later printing is a 1995 copy or that Rush painted this illustration.

Strixhaven Mystical Archive introduced #13 and Japanese-art #76 in April 2021. Each has nonfoil, traditional foil and etched entries. Mark Poole’s Secret Lair #174 followed in June 2021 with nonfoil and foil. Those treatments represent distinct artist and product choices, not a single linear upgrade ladder.

About this guide

Brainstorm first appeared in Ice Age in 1995: ICE #61, a common illustrated by Christopher Rush, available in nonfoil only. This guide compares five collecting families: the Ice Age original, DiTerlizzi’s Masques-era art, Chris Rahn’s later reprints, Mystical Archive’s two art treatments and Mark Poole’s Artist Series. These six selected records let you compare the actual illustration, collector number and surface before opening the live printing archive below.

ARTWORK & FRAME COMPARISON

Which differences change the illustration?

◇

Start with Rush’s ICE #61 and DiTerlizzi’s MMQ #61 if you want to compare two early interpretations. The shared number makes set identification especially useful. The old border by itself cannot tell you which artist or edition is in front of you.

Rahn’s A25 #46 and Poole’s SLD #174 provide two later paintings. Poole’s name belongs to the Artist Series here; it is not the original Brainstorm artist. Compare nonfoil copies first if your main question is which illustration you prefer, then introduce the foil surface separately.

STA #13 credits Justin Hernandez & Alexis Hernandez; STA #76 credits Ayako Ishiguro and is a Japanese-language alternate-art record. This is a new illustration, not merely the global artwork with translated text. When ordering, specify the collector number, language and finish together.

FOILS & SPECIAL TREATMENTS

Choose the printing before its finish

◇

The original ICE #61 is nonfoil-only. MMQ #61 offers a later old-border traditional foil; A25 #46 and SLD #174 have ordinary nonfoil/foil choices. The edition matters even if a listing uses only the word “foil”.

The Mystical Archive records list three finishes. Etched and traditional foil are distinct surfaces, while #13 versus #76 changes the illustration and language family. An etched #13 is not a Japanese alternate-art #76. Use the actual finish field and photographs under ordinary lighting; a static scan or simulated shimmer cannot demonstrate the physical surface.

MANASCOUT RECOMMENDATIONS

Which printing suits your collection?

◇

Original-printing collection: ICE #61 nonfoil is the historical anchor. Check the artist and set code rather than looking for an unavailable Ice Age foil.

Old-border foil deck: MMQ #61 is our first controlled comparison. Hold the artwork constant between its nonfoil and foil before choosing a more elaborate modern frame.

Artist-led binder: put A25 #46 beside SLD #174 to compare Rahn and Poole. The watermark and Artist Series product identity answer different collecting questions.

Archive treatment collection: compare STA #13 with #76 in the same finish first. Then decide whether etched or traditional foil suits that art. The live explorer is the place to extend this shortlist into other reprints, languages and current offers.
