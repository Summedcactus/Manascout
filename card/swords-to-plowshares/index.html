PRINTING HISTORY

Swords to Plowshares across Magic's history

◇

Swords to Plowshares first appeared in Limited Edition Alpha in 1993. Its one-mana exile effect established it as one of white's most recognisable removal spells from the very beginning of Magic: The Gathering.

Since then, the card has been reprinted across many different products and eras, building up a large range of artwork, frames, set identities and finishes.

That long history makes Swords to Plowshares particularly well suited to printing comparison because the same spell has appeared in many very different visual forms.

VERSIONS & TREATMENTS

What changes between Swords to Plowshares printings?

◇

Different Swords to Plowshares printings can vary through artwork, frame design, set symbol, foil finish and other premium treatments while keeping the same core spell.

Older copies preserve the look of early Magic, while newer releases can offer alternative illustrations, modern frames and more collector-focused presentation.

Because the card has such a long reprint history, choosing a version can be as much about visual preference and nostalgia as it is about finding a playable copy.

WHICH ONE?

Which Swords to Plowshares printing should you choose?

◇

For a deck copy: compare the lower-priced available reprints first if your priority is simply adding the card to a deck.

For classic Magic: older-frame versions may appeal most if you prefer the presentation and character of the game's earliest years.

For artwork: Swords to Plowshares is especially worth browsing visually, as its illustrations have changed substantially over time.

For collecting: premium finishes and unusual treatments can make some versions stand out far more clearly than standard reprints.
