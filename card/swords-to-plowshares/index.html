HOW IT WORKS

One mana to exile, with life based on the creature’s power

◇

Swords to Plowshares costs {W}, is an Instant and has a white colour identity. It exiles a target creature, and that creature’s controller gains life equal to its power.

The life calculation uses the creature’s power as it last existed on the battlefield. If the spell’s only target is illegal when it would resolve, the spell does not resolve. Exiling is different from destroying and does not itself cause the creature to die.

Compared with Path to Exile, the defining concession is life rather than an optional basic land. The same current Oracle rules apply to the original Alpha card and later artwork versions.

PRINTING HISTORY

Alpha history, distinct promo art and modern collector branches

◇

The first printing is Limited Edition Alpha (LEA) #40, released on 5 August 1993 at uncommon, illustrated by Jeff A. Menges and available only in nonfoil. #40 is a catalogue number, not a number printed on the original card. Later Beta and Unlimited editions are catalogued as #41; that difference matters when identifying an edition.

Ice Age #54 in 1995 supplies Kaja Foglio artwork, an early alternative to the Menges image. It is a nonfoil edition from the older-frame era.

The 2013 Judge Gift Card J13 #1 is foil-only with Sam Wolfe Connelly art. From the Vault: Twenty V13 #2, also in 2013, uses a different illustration by Terese Nielsen in that product’s foil presentation. Eternal Masters #32 later reuses Nielsen’s painting with nonfoil and foil options.

Strixhaven Mystical Archive in 2021 adds global #10 by Mark Tedin and Japanese alternate-art #73 by Nagano. Their frame, language and finish choices require more precise identification than “Archive Swords.”

Secret Lair #1021 in 2022 belongs to Kamigawa: The Manga: The Cards and credits Ryo Kamei. Fallout in 2024 introduces Alex Konstad artwork on regular #173 and surge-foil #701. These are meaningful later collector branches without requiring every minor Commander reprint in the editorial table.

About this guide

Swords to Plowshares began in Alpha with Jeff A. Menges’ artwork and has accumulated an enormous catalogue since. This guide chooses representative artistic eras: Kaja Foglio’s Ice Age image, a Judge foil, Terese Nielsen’s reprint family, Mystical Archive art and later manga and Fallout treatments. The live explorer remains the place for the exhaustive list.

ARTWORK & FRAMES

The classic image is only one of several long-lived families

◇

Menges and Foglio: Alpha and Ice Age are distinct early illustrations. Shared artwork across an early edition does not by itself identify Alpha; inspect border, corners and the actual product.

Connelly and Nielsen: the Judge and From the Vault cards are both 2013 foils, but they are not the same image. EMA #32 makes Nielsen’s illustration available in a later ordinary reprint with two surface choices.

Tedin and Nagano: STA #10 and #73 are separate Mystical Archive paintings. #73 is the Japanese-language artwork record, rather than simply the Japanese translation of #10.

Kamei and Konstad: SLD #1021 uses manga imagery; Fallout #173 and #701 use Konstad’s crossover illustration. The Fallout surge variant shares its picture with the regular record.

FOILS & PREMIUM FINISHES

A specific foil history, not a finish available on every edition

◇

Alpha #40 and Ice Age #54 are nonfoil-only. Judge J13 #1 and From the Vault V13 #2 are foil-only, with different artists. EMA #32 offers nonfoil and traditional foil.

Both shortlisted Mystical Archive records have nonfoil, traditional foil and etched foil availability. An etched #73 and an ordinary foil #73 share the Japanese painting; finish is not another artwork.

SLD #1021 has nonfoil and traditional foil. Fallout #173 also offers nonfoil and traditional foil; #701 is a separate surge-foil-only record. Do not collapse the traditional and surge surfaces into one undifferentiated “Fallout foil.”

The physical-card preview is useful for identifying art and frames but cannot reproduce the real etched or surge surface. Check the set, number, language and finish on the actual offer.

WHICH PRINTING?

Which Swords to Plowshares printing should you choose?

◇

For the first printing: Alpha #40 is the reference. Beta and Unlimited #41 are different editions, even though their familiar front artwork can look similar in a small image.

For early alternate art: Ice Age #54 gives you Foglio’s image in an older frame. For a later conventional reprint, compare Nielsen’s EMA #32.

For a specific promo: J13 #1 is the Connelly Judge foil; V13 #2 is the Nielsen From the Vault foil. The shared year and reflective finish do not identify the artist.

For Archive art: compare Tedin’s #10 with Nagano’s #73 before deciding on traditional or etched foil. Verify language separately.

For a themed collection: compare manga #1021 and Fallout #173. Choose #701 only if surge foil is specifically part of the Fallout goal.
